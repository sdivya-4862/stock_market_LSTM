# Copy the brain's memory, not its spikes

You can build a capable, private AI companion that runs offline on a phone or laptop today. You can't get there by inventing a new brain-like network and training it from scratch. On consumer hardware, the brain principles that pay off are the ones that reduce how many bytes move per generated token. On-device text generation is limited by memory bandwidth, just as the cortex spends far more energy sending signals than computing with them. Those principles are sparse and conditional computation (mixture-of-experts, activation sparsity), low-precision weights (4-bit, 2-bit, ternary), and recurrent layers with a fixed-size state mixed with a few attention layers. Add the brain's memory architecture on top: a fast "hippocampal" store of episodes beside slow "cortical" weights, writes gated by salience, and "sleep-time" consolidation while the device charges. Every part of that design runs today on open-weight models (Liquid AI's LFM2.5, IBM's Granite 4.0-H, Qwen3.5, Gemma 4) and mature runtimes (llama.cpp, MLX, SQLite vector search). Spiking LLMs and neuromorphic, analog, photonic and organoid chips won't run a phone's main model before roughly 2028. The nearest hardware gain is processing-in-memory DRAM: Samsung showed it tripling an 8B model's token rate, and it could reach AI PCs in 2027. Data efficiency is the least-solved gap. Models trained on a child's 100M words now match a 70B model on grammar but not on knowledge, and even Liquid AI's "liquid" models train on 10–28 trillion tokens. So inherit a pretrained model and make only the personal learning child-efficient. One constraint shapes everything: Claude's weights are not public. Nobody outside Anthropic can rebuild Claude with a brain-like architecture or run it offline, so any new architecture means training or adapting a different, open-weight model. Claude can at most be a development-time teacher that never sees user data. The honest product is a private, memory-centric companion, not a private ChatGPT. Small local models still trail frontier models by 25–39 points on personal-assistant tasks when the surrounding prompts and tools are not tuned for them, and they hallucinate on over 80% of hard factual questions.

## On-device generation hits the same wall the cortex does

The human brain runs about **86 billion neurons** ([Herculano-Houzel 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22723358/)) on roughly 10–23 W ([comparative table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3950304/table/T1)). That is about 20% of the body's energy for 2% of its mass ([Herculano-Houzel 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3046985)). The budget forces sparse activity. Attwell and Laughlin's bottom-up energy accounting predicts that **no more than about 15% of cortical neurons can be active at once** ([Attwell & Laughlin 2001](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1097/00004647-200110000-00001)). Lennie put the ceiling below 1% ([Lennie 2003](https://www2.bcs.rochester.edu/sites/plennie/pdfs/Lennie03a.pdf)), but later work found spikes cheaper than his model assumed, so read 1% as a high-cost scenario ([Howarth et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3390818)). The most useful number for an engineer comes from Levy and Calvert. Cortical *computation* costs about 0.1 W, while long-distance *communication* costs about 3.5 W, so **sending signals costs ~35x more than computing with them** ([Levy & Calvert 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8106317)). The brain is efficient less because its arithmetic is cheap than because it avoids moving data: synapses store weights where they are used, few neurons signal at once, and signals are slow and low-precision. This also makes "brain 20 W versus GPU 700 W" comparisons rhetorical, since the computing share of the brain's budget is a fraction of a watt.

Chips have the same imbalance, only larger. At 45 nm, reading a 32-bit word from DRAM costs about **640 pJ versus 0.1 pJ for a 32-bit integer add, roughly 6,400x** ([Horowitz, ISSCC 2014](https://www.gwern.net/doc/cs/hardware/2014-horowitz-2.pdf)). Generating one token for one user reads every weight of a dense model from memory. Tokens per second is therefore close to memory bandwidth divided by model size. Community llama.cpp benchmarks on Apple silicon show this almost exactly. Llama 2 7B at 4-bit runs at 14.2 tok/s on an M1 (68 GB/s), 24.1 on an M4 (120 GB/s), 31.9 on an M5 (154 GB/s) and **119.9 on an M5 Max (614 GB/s)** ([llama.cpp discussion #4167](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/4167)). By our arithmetic, every result lands at about 75–80% of the bandwidth ceiling. Apple attributes the M5's 19–27% faster local token generation to its extra bandwidth; the new GPU neural accelerators mostly sped up prompt processing ([9to5Mac on Apple ML Research](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/20/apple-shows-how-much-faster-the-m5-runs-local-llms-compared-to-the-m4/)). A chip's NPU TOPS rating barely affects chat speed.

Phones have far less bandwidth. The iPhone 17 Pro's A19 Pro has about 76 GB/s ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2942646/apples-m5-mac-chip-is-just-a-big-a19-pro-and-thats-a-good-thing.html)) and a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 about 85 GB/s ([91mobiles](https://www.91mobiles.com/processor/qualcomm-snapdragon-8-elite-gen-5-pdp)). The same arithmetic caps a 4-bit 3B model (~1.8 GB) at about 45 tok/s and a 4-bit 8B model at 17–19 tok/s. Measurements fit. Llama 3.2 3B ran at **27.2 tok/s on an iPhone Air** ([pocket-ai-lab](https://ananasdda.github.io/pocket-ai-lab/benchmarks/)), and Gemma 4 E4B at about 18 tok/s on CPU and 22 on GPU on a Galaxy S26 Ultra ([Google LiteRT-LM](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert-lm/models/gemma-4)). Heat then erodes these numbers. Sustained runs lost about 15% on a Galaxy S24 Ultra and **41.5% on an iPhone** ([arXiv 2603.23640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23640)). Llama 3.2 3B on a Snapdragon Adreno 830 GPU fell from 14–24 tok/s when cool to 4–13 tok/s under sustained load ([arXiv 2609.30270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30270)). Measured phone energy ranges from 0.257 J/token with PowerInfer-2 to 0.672 J/token with llama.cpp ([PowerInfer-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.06282)). At 20 tok/s that means 5–14 W, enough to explain the throttling. BrainChip's own engineering deck makes the point starkly: a 1B model at 20 tok/s needs about **20 mW of compute but about 2 W of DRAM power** ([BrainChip, 2025](https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/E2W07_Lewis_BrainChip_2025.pdf)).

Two conclusions follow. First, the brain lessons that transfer to a phone are the ones that cut bytes per token: fewer active weights, fewer bits per weight, and no attention cache that grows with every word. Spikes save energy only on event-driven hardware, which phones don't have. Second, running locally is a privacy choice, not an energy one. A 2026 battery study found that moving the same work from a Pixel 8 to a server that batches many users' requests saves about 65% of the energy per token. Only servers that run one request at a time lose to phones ([The Battery Price of edge AI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11940)). A founder should sell "your data never leaves the device," not "greener AI." Most efficiency figures in this field are vendor-reported or estimated rather than measured on phones, and the rest of this report flags them as such.

## Four brain principles have paid off, and most others remain lab results

| Brain principle | AI translation | Strongest evidence | Verdict for a local companion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sparse firing, conditional computation | Mixture-of-experts, activation sparsity | 1.53–1.8x faster decoding without retraining; 47B model on a phone | Use now |
| Complementary learning systems (hippocampus, neocortex, sleep replay) | Retrieval memory, replay, sparse consolidation | Forgetting cut to 11% versus 71% (LoRA) and 89% (full fine-tuning) | Use now |
| Neuromodulation (dopamine, acetylcholine) | Reinforcement learning; salience-gated memory writes | Dopamine matches TD error; plastic networks only on toy tasks | Use as a policy |
| Continuous-time recurrent dynamics | SSMs, linear attention, gated convolutions, CfC | Hybrids match or beat transformers; ~2x CPU speed (vendor) | Use; loose brain link |
| Active inference, free energy | Bayesian object-centric world models | Wins on narrow games and puzzles; company evidence | Planning sub-module, research |
| Cortical columns (Thousand Brains) | Monty sensorimotor learning modules | 98.6% on 77 objects with ~4M parameters | Watch; no language |
| Local, Hebbian, predictive-coding learning | Forward-forward, equilibrium propagation, SoftHebb, μPC | Best ImageNet-224 forward-forward result: 26% top-1 | Don't build on |
| Dendritic computation | Dendritic ANNs, active dendrites | Small image and continual-learning tasks | Don't build on |
| Spikes | Spiking LLMs | Energy gains estimated; need neuromorphic chips | Don't build on for phones |

### Sparsity and memory systems are proven

Sparse activation has the clearest, independently reproduced payoff of any brain principle, because it directly cuts memory traffic. Trained transformers are already sparse: after ReLU, only about 3% of T5-Base's MLP activations are nonzero ([Lazy Neuron, ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06313)). TEAL exploits this without retraining. It reaches 40–50% activation sparsity on 7B–70B Llama and Mistral models and makes **single-user decoding 1.53–1.8x faster** ([TEAL, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14690)). The gains grow on phones when the model doesn't fit in fast memory. PowerInfer-2 served a 47B model on a smartphone at 11.68 tok/s, **on average 25.4x faster than llama.cpp** ([PowerInfer-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06282)). Mixture-of-experts is the mainstream form of the same idea. Bigger claims deserve suspicion. Numenta's 70–123x CPU speedups mix a hardware-generation upgrade with software and have not been independently replicated, and Numenta's own Hot Chips slides note that CPUs need about 90% sparsity just to break even with dense math ([ServeTheHome](https://www.servethehome.com/numenta-has-the-secret-to-ai-inference-on-cpus-like-the-intel-xeon-max/); [Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/customer-spotlight/stories/numenta-transformer-networks-customer-story.html)).

The second proven principle is complementary learning systems. In this theory the neocortex slowly builds structured knowledge, the hippocampus rapidly stores individual experiences, and replay, much of it during sleep, consolidates one into the other ([Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016](https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/KumaranHassabisMcClelland16FinalMS.pdf)). Every part has a working AI counterpart. Retrieval over a store of past episodes plays the hippocampus. Replay remains the strongest general defense against catastrophic forgetting. And sparse consolidation now works at language-model scale. Meta's Sparse Memory Finetuning updates only the memory slots that a new fact activates unusually strongly. While learning a stream of TriviaQA facts, it **cut forgetting on NaturalQuestions to 11%, versus 71% for LoRA and 89% for full fine-tuning** ([Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103)). An independent ICML 2026 re-implementation on Qwen-2.5-0.5B found the trade-off cuts both ways. Sparse Memory Finetuning gained 2.5 points on the new task, versus 4.6 for LoRA and 5.4 for full fine-tuning, but kept forgetting within about one point of the base model ([ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/69392)). For a companion whose general competence must survive years of updates, that is the right trade. Google's Nested Learning pushes the idea toward a continuum of memory modules that update at different rates ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/introducing-nested-learning-a-new-ml-paradigm-for-continual-learning/)). A long-lived learner must also guard against **loss of plasticity**: in continual settings, standard deep networks gradually lose the ability to learn at all. Occasionally reinitializing rarely used units fixes it ([Dohare et al., Nature 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11338828/)).

The third is neuromodulation. Dopamine firing tracks the temporal-difference reward-prediction error ([Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)). That is the most successful convergence between neuroscience and AI, and the root of reinforcement learning. Networks with explicit neuromodulated plasticity work only on small meta-learning tasks ([Backpropamine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10585)). The practical translation is therefore a policy, not a learning rule: write to long-term memory, or schedule consolidation, only when a salience signal is high. That signal can be surprise, explicit user feedback or emotional weight. The fourth principle, recurrent continuous-time dynamics, survives as efficient sequence layers whose brain link is loose (next section).

### Learning rules and dendrites have not scaled

Brain-plausible alternatives to backpropagation remain far behind it. Hinton's forward-forward algorithm reached only **26.21% top-1 on ImageNet** in its first claimed result there ([ASGE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12394)). Hebbian SoftHebb reached 27.3% ([Journé et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11883)). Holomorphic equilibrium propagation matches backprop, but only up to 32×32-pixel ImageNet with a five-layer CNN ([Laborieux & Zenke 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530)). Predictive-coding networks now train 128-layer ResNets, but only on MNIST- and CIFAR-scale tasks ([μPC, NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13124)). The variants that come closest to backprop, such as weight-mirror feedback alignment (23.4% versus 22.9% ResNet-50 error), get there by approximating backprop's gradient more faithfully ([2026 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19995)). No local rule has trained a language model beyond toy scale. On a phone, on-device learning should use ordinary backprop with small adapters while the phone charges. Local rules make sense only on analog or neuromorphic chips that consumers don't own.

Dendrites tell a similar story. A single cortical pyramidal neuron needs a 5–8-layer deep network to emulate at millisecond precision, mostly because of NMDA-driven dendritic nonlinearities ([Beniaguev, Segev & London 2021](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/single-cortical-neurons-as-deep-artificial-neural-networks/); [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-computationally-complex-is-a-single-neuron-20210902/)). So "the brain has N neurons, so we need N parameters" comparisons are off by orders of magnitude. Dendritic ANNs match standard nets with fewer parameters on image tasks ([Chavlis & Poirazi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03708)), and Numenta's active dendrites reduce interference in small continual-learning tests ([Iyer et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2022.846219/full)). In practice, though, choosing a sub-network based on context is what mixture-of-experts routing and task adapters already do at scale.

### Active inference and Thousand Brains win only in narrow worlds

Two flagship brain theories have working systems with striking efficiency numbers in narrow domains. VERSES' active-inference model AXIOM uses no neural networks or backprop. Per VERSES, it beat DreamerV3 on the company's Gameworld 10k arcade benchmark with **0.95M versus 420M parameters** and 39x faster learning ([VERSES](https://www.verses.ai/news/verses-digital-brain-beats-googles-top-ai-at-gameworld-10k-atari-challenge); [AXIOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24784)). The validation report VERSES commissioned is more nuanced. DreamerV3 won one game outright, and AXIOM's margin on two others was within 1% ([Soothsayer report](https://www.verses.ai/hubfs/images/blog/2025-05%20Gameworld/Soothsayer%20VERSES%20Axiom%20Technical%20Validation%20Report.pdf)). The benchmark also appears to be the authors' own. VERSES' Genius solved 100 of 100 Mastermind games against 45% for DeepSeek R1 ([VERSES](https://www.verses.ai/research-summary)), but Mastermind is a constraint-satisfaction puzzle, a poor test for a general language model. The company narrowed its product focus in December 2025 ([VERSES newsletter](https://www.verses.ai/news/verses-monthly-newsletter-december-2025)). The neuroscience behind it is unsettled: the evidence for predictive coding in the brain is real but mixed ([Walsh et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187369)).

The Thousand Brains Project's Monty is a sensorimotor system built from many learning modules modeled on cortical columns, which vote on what they sense. It recognized all **77 objects in the YCB set, including their rotation, at 98.6% accuracy using about 4M parameters**. It needed about 528 million times fewer training FLOPs than a pretrained vision transformer (ViT) ([Leadholm et al., Neural Computation 2026](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO.a.1508); [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04494)). That multiplier mostly reflects the ViT's ImageNet pretraining, the task suits Monty, and the code is an early-stage (API v0) research platform with no language ability ([tbp.monty](https://github.com/thousandbrainsproject/tbp.monty)). For a companion, active inference could serve as a small planning layer above the language model, tracking the user's state and deciding when to ask versus act. Monty is a long-horizon item to watch.

## Hybrids with a little attention beat both transformers and pure recurrences

### Your LSTM's descendants are back, with attention as a crutch

If you have built an LSTM, the biggest architecture story of 2024–2026 will feel familiar: recurrence came back. Mamba-2 and Mamba-3, RWKV-7, Gated DeltaNet and xLSTM's matrix-memory mLSTM are all gated recurrent networks. Each keeps a fixed-size, matrix-valued state (sometimes called "fast weights") that updates with every token. They therefore generate with **constant memory and constant time per token**, instead of a cache that grows with every word. Their weakness is the LSTM's old weakness: a fixed-size state cannot hold arbitrary history verbatim. A two-layer transformer can copy exponentially long strings. Any state-space model fails at copying unless its state grows with sequence length, and in experiments Mamba needed about 100x more data to learn copying ([Jelassi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)). NVIDIA's controlled 8B study found pure SSMs matched transformers on many tasks but lagged on copying, in-context learning and 5-shot MMLU. Yet an **8B hybrid with just 7% attention layers (43% Mamba-2, 50% MLP) beat the transformer on all 12 short-context benchmarks, including +3.5 points on 5-shot MMLU** ([Waleffe et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07887)). A 2025 systematic study recommends three to six linear layers per full-attention layer ([arXiv 2507.06457](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06457v1)). A companion has to quote names, dates and earlier conversations exactly. That makes pure recurrence risky, and hybrids the answer.

Industry has converged on this design. **NVIDIA's Nemotron-H** keeps about 8% of its layers as attention and reports up to 3x the throughput of similar transformers at long context ([Nemotron-H](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03624v1)). **IBM's Granite 4.0** uses a reported 9:1 ratio of Mamba-2 to attention layers, claims over 70% less RAM for long inputs ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/new/announcements/ibm-granite-4-0-hyper-efficient-high-performance-hybrid-models)), and ships 350M and ~1.5B "Nano" hybrids for devices ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-4-nano)). **TII's Falcon-H1** runs attention and SSM heads side by side in each layer and claims its 0.5B model rivals typical 2024 7B models ([Falcon-H1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448)), while **Zyphra's Zamba2** reports 6x less key-value-cache memory than comparable transformers ([Zamba2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15242v1)). At larger scale, **Alibaba's Qwen3-Next** pairs Gated DeltaNet and attention layers 3:1 and runs 10x faster than Qwen3-32B at 128k context, though independent testers placed its quality mid-pack ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/alibabas-new-model-uses-hybrid-attention-layers-and-a-sparse-moe-architecture-for-speed-and-performance)).

The newest pure recurrence, Mamba-3 (an ICLR 2026 oral paper), matches Mamba-2's perplexity with half the state and solves state-tracking tasks Mamba-2 cannot. It has no large open chat model yet ([Mamba-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569)). Most hybrid speedups were measured on datacenter GPUs at 32k–128k-token contexts. At typical chat lengths of 2–8k tokens on a phone they shrink. IBM ships transformer-only Nano variants precisely because hybrid kernels lag in some runtimes.

Two recurrent families matter specifically to you. **xLSTM 7B** comes from the group of LSTM co-inventor Sepp Hochreiter. It performs comparably to similar-size LLMs, was the fastest 7B model its authors tested, and keeps generation speed constant as the prompt grows ([xLSTM 7B, ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13427)). It is the natural on-ramp for your own experiments, but llama.cpp support is unconfirmed, so it can't ship on phones yet. **RWKV-7** set a 3B-class state of the art on multilingual tasks with constant memory, under an Apache 2.0 license ([RWKV-7](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2503.14456)). Community builds at 1.5–13.3B run in Ollama ([Ollama](https://ollama.com/shoumenchougou/rwkv7-g1l)). It also supports "state tuning", which personalizes the recurrent state without touching the weights ([arXiv 2504.05097](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05097)). But its G1 checkpoints are not safety-aligned assistants, and users informally report sharp forgetting beyond 8–16k tokens ([Hugging Face discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.14456)).

### "Liquid" models win on phones, but not because of liquid neurons

Liquid time-constant (LTC) and closed-form continuous-time (CfC) networks are genuinely brain-inspired. They are tiny RNNs based on differential equations, modeled on the neurons of the worm *C. elegans*, and they excel at time series and control. CfC runs at least 10x faster than its differential-equation predecessors ([Hasani et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13898)). Liquid AI's commercial LFM2 models are something else. An architecture search benchmarked on actual phone and laptop CPUs converged on **10 gated short-convolution blocks plus 6 grouped-query-attention blocks, with no SSM or liquid-neuron layers** ([LFM2 technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)). They are nonetheless the best-evidenced phone models:

- Liquid reports LFM2-1.2B against the larger Qwen3-1.7B at 55.2 vs 59.1 on MMLU, 58.3 vs 51.4 on GSM8K and 74.9 vs 74.0 on IFEval, with up to 2x faster CPU prefill and decode ([LFM2-1.2B model card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2-1.2B/blob/26bb21ffbfe2d9527e007758ddddb3700955d2af/README.md)).
- LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct decodes at **116 tok/s on a laptop CPU**, an AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 ([Liquid AI](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/introducing-lfm2-5-the-next-generation-of-on-device-ai)).
- LFM2.5-24B-A2B runs about 2B active parameters at 184 tok/s on an M5 Max ([Liquid AI](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-24b-a2b)).

All of these are vendor numbers with no independent replication. And LFM2.5-350M was trained on **28 trillion tokens** ([Liquid AI](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-350m-no-size-left-behind)): cheap to run, but no more data-efficient than any other LLM. CfC-style layers remain a good fit for small sensor models on a phone, such as activity or physiology tracking, not for language.

### Mixture-of-experts and ternary weights shrink what each token touches

Mixture-of-experts cuts compute per token but not storage, because every expert must sit in RAM or be streamed from flash. OpenAI's gpt-oss-20b (21B total, 3.6B active) fits in about 16 GB with 4-bit MXFP4 ([vLLM](https://recipes.vllm.ai/openai/gpt-oss-20b)). Yet a 16 GB Mac mini was the slowest machine in one independent test, because that memory is shared with the system ([Micro Center](https://www.microcenter.com/site/mc-news/article/can-your-pc-run-gpt-oss-ai.aspx)). Moving unused experts out to flash storage costs **4–20x latency** ([CoSMoEs](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.00245)). The practical phone rule is to keep the total model under about 4–5 GB at 4-bit. For a mixture-of-experts model, that means about 8B total parameters with 1–1.5B active, such as LFM2-8B-A1B (8.3B/1.5B) or Granite-4.0-H-Tiny (7B/1B). Laptops with 32 GB or more can run gpt-oss-20b or other 20–30B mixture-of-experts models. Google's Gemma 3n gets a similar effect differently: it keeps per-layer embeddings on the CPU, so models with 5B and 8B raw parameters run in 2–3 GB ([Google](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemma-3n-developer-guide/)).

Low-bit weights are the largest single memory lever. Microsoft's **BitNet b1.58 2B4T** is the only ternary LLM trained natively at production quality; its weights are −1, 0 or +1. It needs 0.4 GB of non-embedding memory, versus 0.7 GB for Qwen2.5-1.5B at INT4 and 2.6 GB at bf16. It decodes on a laptop CPU at **29 ms per token versus 65 ms**. Its 11-benchmark average is 54.2 versus 55.2, but on knowledge-heavy MMLU it scores 53.2 versus 60.3 ([BitNet 2B4T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12285); [model card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/bitnet-b1.58-2B-4T)). Its energy figures (0.028 J versus 0.347 J) come from an operation-count model, not a power meter. The gains appear only with the dedicated bitnet.cpp runtime ([Hugging Face docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/v5.13.1/model_doc/bitnet); [bitnet.cpp](https://github.com/microsoft/BitNet)). The ecosystem is thin, with one official 2B model, but hardware is moving toward it. MediaTek's Dimensity 9500 supports BitNet 1.58-bit models on its NPU ([MediaTek](https://www.mediatek.com/dimensity-9500)), and Apple ships its ~3B on-device model with 2-bit quantization-aware training ([Apple 2025 tech report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13575)). For ordinary deployments, standard 4-bit quantization (llama.cpp's Q4_K_M format) of an 8B model adds only ~0.18 perplexity ([llama.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)). Models of 1–3B parameters need checkpoints trained for quantization, because generic 4-bit conversion after training hurts them ([ExecuTorch](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/external/executorch/+/HEAD/examples/models/llama)).

### Spiking LLMs are converted transformers running on GPUs

Spiking language models grew from SpikeGPT's 216M parameters in 2023 ([SpikeGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.13939v5)) to the Chinese Academy of Sciences' SpikingBrain-7B and SpikingBrain-76B in September 2025. Both were built by converting the existing Qwen2.5-7B model with about 150B tokens of further pretraining ([SpikingBrain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276)). The 7B keeps roughly 90% of its base model's accuracy ([Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/spikingbrain-models)) with 69% activation sparsity. It claims over 100x faster time-to-first-token at a 4M-token context, but that advantage comes from linear attention, not spikes. Its claimed 97.7% energy reduction is a per-operation estimate. The official repository calls its spikes **"pseudo-spiking" at the tensor level and notes that true event-driven spiking "requires asynchronous hardware"** ([GitHub](https://github.com/BICLab/SpikingBrain-7B)). A compressed 1B version decoded 15.39x faster than Llama-3.2-1B on a mobile CPU framework at a 256k-token context, a gap that is far smaller at chat lengths ([36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3458000417642121)). SpikingBrain 2.0 (April 2026) recovered most of Qwen3-4B's capability in a 5B model using **under 7,000 A100 GPU-hours** ([SpikingBrain 2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22575v1)). Whatever one thinks of the spikes, that shows converting an open model into a more efficient architecture is affordable for a funded startup. The only language model measured on actual neuromorphic hardware is a 370M MatMul-free model on Intel Loihi 2. Preliminary results show up to 3x the throughput and half the energy of a transformer on an edge GPU ([Abreu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18002)).

### Test-time memory is the most brain-like idea, and not yet shippable

The deepest brain parallel in current architectures is associative memory. The update rule of modern Hopfield networks *is* transformer attention, with storage capacity exponential in dimension ([Ramsauer et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)). Recurrent states act as fast-weight memories. And a new family of models updates its memory by gradient descent *while reading*:

- **TTT layers** keep improving perplexity with more context, where Mamba plateaus after 16k tokens ([Sun et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)).
- **Google's Titans** pairs attention as short-term memory with a neural long-term memory that memorizes "surprising" inputs at test time. It scales past 2M tokens ([Titans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663); [Google](https://research.google/blog/titans-miras-helping-ai-have-long-term-memory/)).
- **ATLAS** reports +80% accuracy on the BABILong benchmark at 10M tokens ([ATLAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735)).
- **HOLA**, a 2026 "hippocampus for linear attention", explicitly implements complementary learning systems: an exact cache of surprising tokens sits beside a compressed recurrent state ([HOLA](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.02303.md)).
- **Meta's memory layers** add large sparse key-value tables, which add parameters without adding compute ([Memory Layers at Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09764)).

The catch: no official open Titans or ATLAS weights exist, and results are at 1B scale or below. When Hugging Face tried to reproduce Google's related Infini-attention, **performance fell as compressed memories accumulated** ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/infini-attention)). Today, the practical hippocampus is retrieval over a local database.

### JEPA improves representations but does not generate text

Yann LeCun's JEPA approach predicts in embedding space rather than in tokens or pixels. V-JEPA 2, a 1.2B video world model, scores 77.3 on the Something-Something v2 benchmark. After training on under 62 hours of unlabeled robot video, it plans pick-and-place moves zero-shot on real robot arms ([Meta](https://ai.meta.com/blog/v-jepa-2-world-model-benchmarks/); [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985)). For language, JEPA so far works only as an extra training loss. LLM-JEPA adds embedding prediction on top of next-token training and improved fine-tuning results across Llama 3, Gemma 2 and OLMo ([LLM-JEPA, ICLR 2026](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2509.14252)). That makes it a cheap trick to try when fine-tuning a companion, and a candidate camera encoder later, not a chat backbone.

| Family | Best open models (2025–26) | Versus same-size transformer | Runs on phone/laptop today | Role in a companion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SSM or linear-attention hybrids | Granite 4.0-H (incl. Nano), Falcon-H1, Nemotron-H, Zamba2 | Equal or better quality; much smaller KV cache; 2–3x long-context throughput (vendor) | llama.cpp, MLX | Backbone candidate |
| Gated convolution plus attention ("liquid" LFM2/2.5) | LFM2.5 350M to 24B-A2B | About one size class better per parameter; ~2x CPU speed (vendor) | llama.cpp, MLX, ExecuTorch | Backbone candidate; best phone evidence |
| Pure recurrence | RWKV-7 G1, xLSTM-7b, Falcon Mamba 7B | Comparable on many tasks; weak at copying and recall | RWKV via Ollama; xLSTM unconfirmed | Experiments, state tuning |
| Mixture-of-experts | LFM2-8B-A1B, Granite-4.0-H-Tiny, gpt-oss-20b | More quality per active parameter; no storage savings | llama.cpp, MLX | ~8B total on phones, 20–30B on laptops |

## Brain-like chips stay in the lab until about 2028, while smarter memory arrives first

| Hardware | Largest language-model result | Evidence | Consumer outlook |
|---|---|---|---|
| IBM NorthPole | 3B on 16 cards; three 8B instances on a 288-card, 30 kW rack | IBM-measured | Research prototype; low latency, not phones |
| Intel Loihi 2 | 370M MatMul-free model | Preliminary | Not commercial; "Loihi 3" unconfirmed |
| BrainChip, Innatera | Sub-1B demos; sensing under 1 mW | Vendor | Always-on sensing co-processor around 2027 |
| Analog in-memory (IBM PCM, EnCharge, Mythic) | Speech and CNNs on chip; LLMs only simulated | Mixed | No accuracy-matched LLM yet |
| Processing-in-memory DRAM | Llama 3.1 8B at 3.01x tokens/s | Vendor, preliminary | AI PCs around 2027; phones later |
| Photonic | BERT and ResNet (accuracy disputed) | Measured | Data center first; consumer unlikely before 2030 |
| Organoid | Speaker identification at 78%; game demos | Measured, tiny | Not viable; ethics unresolved |

Neuromorphic chips have run language models, but only small ones, or large ones spread across many chips. IBM's NorthPole interleaves memory and compute on a 12 nm die. It ran a 3B model across 16 PCIe cards at under 1 ms per token, with **72.7x better tokens per second per watt than an H100** at the H100's lowest-latency setting ([IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/blog/northpole-llm-inference-results); [HPEC 2024 paper](https://modha.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/NorthPole_HPEC_LLM_2024.pdf)). Its 2025 rack serves three Granite 8B instances from 288 cards drawing 30 kW ([arXiv 2511.15950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15950)). Intel's Hala Point system packs 1,152 Loihi 2 chips into a 2,600 W chassis ([Intel](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai)), and Loihi 2 is not commercially available ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/?p=171959)). Specifications for a "Loihi 3" have circulated since January 2026, but they trace to syndicated and AI-written news, not to any Intel source ([TokenRing syndication](https://investor.wedbush.com/wedbush/article/tokenring-2026-1-19-the-brain-like-revolution-intels-loihi-3-and-the-dawn-of-real-time-neuromorphic-edge-ai)).

These chips win because they keep weights in on-chip SRAM, so the number of chips grows with model size. A phone die cannot hold an 8B model's 4–5 GB of 4-bit weights in SRAM. Their headline multipliers are also measured on one request at a time, exactly where GPUs are least efficient. Our rough arithmetic from IBM's rack figures puts NorthPole near 1–3 J/token at rack level. A batched H100 manages ~0.02–0.2 J/token ([SIMPLE Law](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03330)), which suggests NorthPole's real advantage is latency. That estimate is uncertain, because it isn't clear whether IBM's 30 kW figure is measured or provisioned, or how it counts users.

Neuromorphic chips built for consumer devices target milliwatt sensing instead. BrainChip's Akida Pico runs wake-word and keyword detection on under 1 mW ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241001993143/en)). Innatera's Pulsar detects presence by radar on 600 µW ([EEJournal](https://www.eejournal.com/industry_news/innatera-unveils-pulsar-the-worlds-first-mass-market-neuromorphic-microcontroller-for-the-sensor-edge/)). BrainChip's Sequora chip design for generative AI preliminarily runs models under 420M parameters at 20 tok/s ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/brainchip-launches-sequora-for-low-power-generative-ai-at-the-edge/)), far below what a capable companion needs. A neuromorphic always-on co-processor for wake words and context sensing is plausible in phones and wearables by 2027. A neuromorphic chip running a phone's main conversational model before about 2028–2030 appears on no vendor roadmap.

Analog in-memory computing does matrix math inside the memory array itself. It has strong measured results on small networks and none on language models. IBM's 14 nm phase-change-memory chip reached 12.4 TOPS/W and an estimated 14x energy-efficiency gain on speech recognition ([Nature 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06337-5)). But every analog result at LLM scale is a simulation ([IBM 3D analog MoE](https://www.research.ibm.com/publications/efficient-scaling-of-large-language-models-with-mixture-of-experts-and-3d-analog-in-memory-computing)). Transformers mapped entirely onto 2-bit memristor (RRAM) cells lose 40 points on one BERT benchmark ([arXiv 2506.00020](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00020v1)). Startups report only their own numbers. EnCharge's EN100 laptop card claims 200+ TOPS at 8.25 W, with no public LLM benchmark ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250529108055/en)). Mythic raised $125M on a claimed "100x energy advantage" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251217546249/en/Mythic-to-Challenge-AIs-GPU-Pantheon-with-100x-Energy-Advantage-and-Oversubscribed-$125M-Raise)).

The hardware change most likely to help a companion is the most mundane: putting logic inside DRAM, known as processing-in-memory (PIM). At Hot Chips 2026, Samsung showed LPDDR5X-PIM delivering **3.01x the tokens per second on Llama 3.1 8B**. It uses about 614 GB/s of bandwidth inside the memory, versus 76.8 GB/s at the external interface ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/hot-chips-2026-samsung-makes-lpddr5x-smart-with-logic-unit-in-memory-lpddr5x-pim-is-3-01x-faster-than-lpddr5x-in-ai-inference-with-8x-the-bandwidth)). A JEDEC LPDDR6-PIM standard is nearly finished ([Newstomato](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=1300001)), and TrendForce reports PIM could reach AI PCs in 2027 ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/08/26/news-samsungs-4nm-gaia-could-mark-first-pim-commercialization-in-ai-pcs-mass-production-as-early-as-2027/)). This is the brain's "compute where the weights are" principle in commercial form. The figures are preliminary and vendor-reported, and the gains depend on chip and runtime support that hasn't been announced.

Photonic and organoid computing are further away still. Lightmatter's photonic processor, published in Nature in 2025, ran ResNet, BERT and Atari reinforcement learning at 65.5 trillion operations per second on 78 W ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08854-x)). Lightmatter claims near-FP32 accuracy, but heise reported accuracy "just under 30%" on natural-language questions ([heise](https://heise.de/-10354733)). Lightmatter's shipping products are optical interconnects ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/01/lightmatter_photonics_passage/)), and faster multiplication does nothing about fetching weights. Organoid computers are real and purchasable. Cortical Labs' CL1 costs about $35,000, or $300 a week in the cloud, and its neurons live about six months ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/biological-computer-for-sale); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/what-a-deal-you-can-now-rent-human-brain-cells-on-a-silicon-chip-for-7-days-for-less-than-a-nintendo-switch-2)). But the best published task is identifying speakers of Japanese vowels at 78% accuracy, worse than a conventional ANN ([Brainoware, Nature Electronics 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41928-023-01069-w)). Claims of "a million times less power" exclude incubators and life support ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/worlds-first-bioprocessor-uses-16-human-brain-organoids-for-a-million-times-less-power-consumption-than-a-digital-chip)), and ethical governance is still only being proposed ([Frontiers in AI](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1307613/full)).

## Children learn from 100 million words, but small models still need trillions

Children reach adult-like grammar from tens of millions of words, and hear fewer than 100 million by early adolescence. LLMs "typically require 3 or 4 orders of magnitude more data" ([BabyLM organizers](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08165v1)); Llama 3 alone trained on over 15 trillion tokens ([Meta](https://ai.meta.com/blog/meta-llama-3/)). Michael Frank lists four candidate explanations for the gap: innate or evolved structure, prior conceptual knowledge, grounding in vision and touch, and social interaction ([Frank 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364661323002036)). One clean experiment narrows the field. Speech directed at children turns out not to be special training data, because synthetic child-style dialogue beat real transcripts. The child's edge therefore most likely lies in its learning algorithm, not its input ([Feng, Goodman & Frank, EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1231.pdf)). The comparison isn't like-for-like either. Children get thousands of hours of synchronized vision, touch and social feedback, evolution supplies a prior, and they learn one language rather than the world's text and code.

BabyLM, a research competition that caps training at 10M or 100M words, has run three rounds:

- **The reliable wins came from architecture and training-objective changes**: LTG-BERT and ELC-BERT in 2023, the hybrid of next-word and masked-word training called GPT-BERT in 2024, and adaptive masking in 2025.
- **Child-like curricula didn't help.** Ordering training data like child development was tried by 42% of 2024 teams and "did not tend to produce high scores" ([2024 findings](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05149)). Adding images failed to beat text-only baselines two years running ([2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)).
- **The best 2025 models matched a 70B model on grammar only.** The top 100M-word models were **"comparable to a Llama 70B parameter model" on the BLiMP grammaticality test** ([2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)). That covers grammar, not knowledge or reasoning, and it was bought with compute: the 2023 winners trained for more than 450 passes over 100M words and more than 2,000 over 10M.

Grounded learning works in miniature. A contrastive model trained on 61 hours of one child's head-camera video learned many word–object pairings that generalized beyond the child's own toys ([Vong et al., Science 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi1374)). The most companion-relevant signal is BabyLM's new Interaction track. One paper there reports that **1M words of interactive feedback from a teacher model improved storytelling as much as 410M words of passive next-word prediction** ([BabyLM 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.32v1.pdf)). It is a single workshop result on one skill, but it points to learning from dialogue feedback as the child-like advantage worth chasing.

Curated and synthetic data stretch small models further in narrow domains. Models under 10M parameters write fluent children's stories when trained on a synthetic corpus limited to a 3-year-old's vocabulary ([TinyStories](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07759v1)). Microsoft's 14B Phi-4, trained mostly on synthetic data, beat its teacher GPT-4o on the GPQA science benchmark (56.1 vs 50.6) but trailed on broad-knowledge MMLU (84.8 vs 88.1) ([Phi-4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08905v1)). When data is scarce, repeating it for up to about four passes is nearly as good as fresh data; after that the returns fade toward zero ([Muennighoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264)).

For a companion, the implication is clear. Don't try to learn language from child-scale data. Inherit a pretrained model and make the *personal* phase child-efficient. Retrieval and in-context memory need zero training examples and work from the first conversation. Personalization stored in model weights keeps shrinking. The per-user LoRA method OPPU needs about 4.2M parameters per user, while 2026 compact user representations match LoRA on the LoCoMo benchmark with about 120x fewer trainable parameters ([arXiv 2606.04547](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04547v2)). Google researchers instead generate a user's LoRA from their data with a hypernetwork, rather than training it on the phone ([arXiv 2609.24979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24979)). Interactive feedback, teacher-generated paraphrases of the user's own facts, and a few passes of replay complete the recipe.

No source we found says how many personal examples weight-based personalization needs before it beats retrieval alone. Your team should measure that crossover. Personal adapters also break when the base model is upgraded ([Heirloom](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00650)). Keep the personal state mainly in a model-agnostic form, such as text memories and preferences, that can be re-distilled into each new base model.

## A small team can ship a cortex, a hippocampus and a sleep cycle today

### Claude can teach the companion but cannot be the companion

Claude's weights are not public. Anthropic offers Claude only through its own apps and API, so no outside team can give Claude a brain-like architecture, change its internals, or run it offline on a phone. Building "a brain-like architecture for Claude" is therefore not an available project. Any new architecture means training or adapting a different model, and for a small team that means starting from open weights. The strongest licensed candidates as of late 2026 are:

- **Qwen3.5** (0.8B–9B): Alibaba has released open weights under Apache 2.0 since Qwen3, but check each repository ([license guide](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/open_weight_model_license_guide.html)).
- **Gemma 4 E2B/E4B**: Apache 2.0 since April 2026 ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemma-4/)).
- **Phi-4-mini** (3.8B): MIT license ([tinyweights](https://tinyweights.dev/posts/best-small-language-models-2026/)).
- **IBM Granite 4.0-H**: the hybrid family covered above.
- **Liquid's LFM2.5**: an "open license based on Apache 2.0" whose commercial terms need checking ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/mit-offshoot-liquid-ai-releases-blueprint-for-enterprise-grade-small-model)).

Claude or another frontier model can still help at *development* time, subject to the provider's usage terms. It can generate synthetic training and test data, grade outputs, or tune the local model's prompts and tool definitions offline. Stanford's OpenJarvis did exactly this, using frontier models offline to propose improvements to a local assistant's prompts and tool specifications ([OpenJarvis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17172)). The condition is that no user's personal data ever reaches the cloud model. In a "never shares" product, a cloud model is a teacher in the factory, never a participant in a user's life.

### The recommended architecture

| Layer | Brain analogue | What works today | Sizing and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base language model | Neocortex (slow weights) | Open-weight hybrid or efficient model: LFM2.5, Granite-4.0-H, Gemma 4 E2B/E4B, Qwen3.5 | Phone: 1–4B dense, or ~8B mixture-of-experts with 1–1.5B active, 4-bit (~1–4 GB). Laptop: 9B dense or 20–30B mixture-of-experts |
| Working memory | Prefrontal working memory | Short 4–16k-token context; hybrid layers keep state small | No 128k raw transcripts |
| Episodic and semantic memory | Hippocampus | One encrypted SQLite file: raw episodes, extracted facts with valid-from and valid-to dates, sqlite-vec embeddings plus full-text search, an always-in-prompt profile | Every answer about the user cites a memory |
| Salience gate | Dopamine, acetylcholine | Write and consolidate only on surprise, explicit feedback or user confirmation; tag each memory's source | Also the main defense against memory poisoning |
| Consolidation | Sleep replay | Charging-time job: merge duplicates, retire contradicted facts, summarize, refresh profile; periodic small LoRA or state tuning on the user's own laptop, with replay | Adapters for style and tone; facts stay in memory |
| Voice and senses | Sensory cortex | Moonshine streaming speech recognition, Kokoro-82M speech synthesis; optional vision via Gemma 4 | Full local voice loop in ~3–4 GB on a recent phone |
| Executive control | Prefrontal cortex, basal ganglia | Small typed tool set; user confirms any send, pay or delete; prompts and tools tuned for the local model | Active-inference-style user modeling is a research add-on |

The base model is the most replaceable layer, so choose it by measurement, not brand. At 4-bit, Qwen3.5-9B needs about 6 GB, the 4B about 3 GB, and the 2B under 2 GB ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)). Hybrids and LFM2.5 promise faster CPU decoding, but no independent study compares hybrid, transformer, BitNet and RWKV models on the same phone for both speed and joules per token. That missing benchmark is the most decision-relevant gap in this field, and a team can build it in a week with llama-bench and a USB power meter. On iOS, Apple's WWDC26 announcements opened its Foundation Models framework to other models and added Core AI and MLX paths for running your own local model ([WWDC26 session 339](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/339/)). On Android, llama.cpp or Google's LiteRT-LM are the portable options.

The memory layer is where the product wins or loses:

- **Copy proven designs into one database.** Use the Zep/Graphiti idea of facts that are marked invalid rather than deleted, stored with both real-world and system timestamps ([comparison](https://medium.com/@wasowski.jarek/i-compared-5-ai-agent-memory-systems-across-6-dimensions-none-wins-6a658335ed0a)). Add Letta's "sleep-time compute", in which a background agent reorganizes memory while the main agent is idle ([arXiv 2504.13171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171)). Both fit in one SQLite file with a vector extension.
- **Test against LongMemEval, with a local model doing the reading.** LongMemEval checks five abilities: extraction, reasoning across sessions, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstaining when the answer isn't known. Even ChatGPT's GPT-4o memory loses 37% against reading the full history directly ([LongMemEval, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)).
- **Distrust vendor scores.** Memory vendors claim 90%+ ([Mem0](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026)), but one independent run of the open-source Mem0 scored 32.4% ([dev.to](https://dev.to/everest_an/-i-benchmarked-ai-agent-memory-in-2026-and-the-numbers-tell-a-different-story-than-the-marketing-2ae4)). A 2026 preprint's title claims 95% with a locally run 4-bit 27B reader ([arXiv 2610.05343](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05343)), which is laptop or workstation class, not phone class.

Consolidation should run on hardware the user already owns. LoRA fine-tuning of a 3B model fits in 1.59 GB of peak memory on a 12 GB phone ([arXiv 2606.19528](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19528v1)), but one epoch over 1,000 examples is projected at about 1.2–1.3 hours ([AQLoRA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23816)). The better design runs adapter training overnight on the user's laptop or desktop with MLX, then syncs the adapter to the phone over an end-to-end-encrypted channel. That makes a two-tier, all-personal-hardware system. OpenJarvis's best local results came from a large local model: Qwen3.5-122B came within 3.2 points of the best cloud model ([OpenJarvis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17172)), which argues for letting a home machine handle the hard thinking.

For voice, the vendor reports that Moonshine's 245M-parameter streaming model beats Whisper Large v3's 1.5B parameters on word error rate (6.65% vs 7.44%) at a fraction of the latency ([Moonshine README](https://github.com/grisuno/moonshine)). Kokoro-82M speaks a 3-second clip in 0.7–0.9 s on an iPhone 15 Pro Max ([Kokoro Core ML](https://huggingface.co/mattmireles/kokoro-coreml)).

### Sell a private memory, not a private oracle

Small models' limits set the product boundary. Qwen3.5-4B and 9B hallucinate on 80–82% of the AA-Omniscience factual test ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)). OpenJarvis tested what happens when existing personal-AI setups swap Claude Opus 4.6 for Qwen3.5-9B. Accuracy dropped **25–39 percentage points**, and re-tuning the prompts and tool definitions for the small model narrowed the drop to 5.6–16.5 points ([OpenJarvis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17172)). Usable context is also shorter than advertised. On the NoLiMa benchmark, which avoids literal word overlap between question and answer, 11 models fell below half their short-context score at 32k tokens ([NoLiMa](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05167v3)). Apple paused its AI news notification summaries in January 2025 after they produced false headlines ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/16/apple-pauses-ai-notification-summaries-for-news-after-generating-false-alerts)), a public example of the failure mode. The design rules follow:

- Answer questions about the user only from retrieved memories, and show the source.
- Decline world-knowledge questions that no local document covers. With no internet connection, current events are out of scope.
- Keep tool use small, well-typed and confirmed by the user.

### Privacy fails through memory and through the company, not the model

The real attack surface is memory. The MINJA attack plants malicious records in an agent's memory using only ordinary queries, with **over 95% injection success** in idealized tests ([MINJA](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.03704)), and OWASP now lists memory and context poisoning among its top agentic risks ([vectorize.io](https://vectorize.io/articles/ai-memory-poisoning)). The companion should therefore:

- Tag every memory with its source: something the user said, something the model inferred, or content from a third party.
- Never let emails, web pages or other people's messages write instructions or profile facts without the user's confirmation.
- Encrypt the memory database with a key held in the phone's secure hardware (Apple's Secure Enclave, Android's StrongBox).
- Treat personal adapters as sensitive data. LoRA memorizes far less than full fine-tuning ([arXiv 2506.20856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20856)), but memorization rises with adapter size, known as rank ([arXiv 2507.21009](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21009v1)).

The other failure mode is corporate. Meta acquired Limitless, formerly Rewind, in December 2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/05/meta-limitless-ai-wearable.html)), and the Rewind Mac app was shut down ([hedy.ai](https://www.hedy.ai/post/meta-acquires-limitless-ai-privacy/)). A "never shares" promise survives an acquisition only if users can export their data in open formats and, ideally, the client code is open source. Two cloud features would bend that promise and should be strictly opt-in. A cloud fallback should meet Apple's Private Cloud Compute standard: stateless processing, hardware attestation and a public transparency log ([Apple Security Research](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/)). Federated learning with differential privacy, as in Google's Gboard ([arXiv 2305.18465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18465)), can improve the shared base model without raw data leaving the phone, but it still shares model updates.

A team of a few engineers can assemble everything in the architecture table from existing components. What sets the product apart is memory quality, consolidation, and prompts and tools tuned for the local model, all measured with a local model on a LongMemEval-style test. The one research bet worth funding early is a custom efficient base model, made by converting or distilling an open model rather than training from scratch. Llamba already distills Llama-3.x into Mamba-style models, reaching 60.0 on MMLU at 8B, and ships an MLX build that runs on iPhones and MacBooks with nearly constant memory ([Llamba](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14458)). This is where your recurrent-network experience pays off most, and its cost is now known and bounded.

## Conclusion

Your instinct to make the companion brain-like is right, but at the wrong level. The brain lessons that pay off sit at the level of the whole system: separate fast and slow memories, gated writes, sleep-like consolidation, and touching only the weights a token needs. The neuron-level lessons, such as spikes, Hebbian rules and dendrites, need hardware consumers won't own before the end of the decade. The cortex-hippocampus-sleep design also ports to whatever hardware arrives. Processing-in-memory DRAM, BitNet-capable NPUs and 2-bit quantization all reward the same choices: few active weights, few bits and a fixed-size state. A design built for bytes per token today will ride each hardware generation rather than wait for neuromorphic chips.

The durable advantage is not the model. Open base models turn over every few months: Qwen3.5 arrived in March 2026 and Gemma 4 in April. What compounds is each person's memory, kept model-agnostic, inspectable and exportable, together with the consolidation process that turns a year of conversations into a model that knows that person. That asset is also the hardest for cloud incumbents to match credibly under a "never shares" promise. The Limitless shutdown shows the promise is only as strong as its survival plan if the company is sold. Build the memory so it outlives both the base model and the company.
