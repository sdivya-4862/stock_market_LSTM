# Brain Principles Behind Biological Efficiency and the State of AI Methods Implementing Them (as of Nov 2026)

Source-status tags used throughout: **[PR]** peer-reviewed (journal or main-track conference), **[PP]** preprint / not peer-reviewed, **[CO]** company or vendor claim (not independently benchmarked unless stated), **[PK]** classic reference cited from prior knowledge, not re-fetched in this session (URL/DOI believed correct; verify before quoting exact numbers). ## 1. Brain energy budget and why it is efficient (~20 W, neuron counts, firing sparsity, spike cost vs. data movement, memory-compute colocation)

### Takeaway
The human brain runs on roughly 10–23 W (≈20% of body energy at ≈2% of body mass) with ~86 billion neurons; energy limits force very sparse activity (estimates range from ≤15% to <1% of neurons substantially active at once). Strikingly, even inside the cortex, *communication* (sending spikes along axons) costs ~35x more energy than *computation* — the same lesson as in chips, where fetching a word from DRAM costs ~1,000–6,000x more than an arithmetic operation. Synapses store weights where they are used, avoiding the von Neumann memory-shuttle cost that dominates on-device LLM inference.

### Cited Findings
- **Neuron count:** ~86 billion neurons in the human brain (isotropic-fractionator counts; vs. the folk figure of 100 billion); human brain is a "scaled-up primate brain" with ~1:1 neuron:non-neuronal cell ratio. [PR, 2012] — [Herculano-Houzel 2012, PNAS (PubMed record)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22723358/); [NPR interview transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/384949670)
- **Energy share:** The brain uses ~20% of the body's energy at ~2% of body mass; Herculano-Houzel argues this is explained by neuron count with a roughly fixed energy budget per neuron. [PR, 2011] — [Herculano-Houzel 2011, PLoS ONE (PMC3046985)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3046985)
- **Wattage:** Commonly cited as ~20 W; one comparative table lists 10–23 W for the human brain. I found no single primary measurement source for "20 W" in this session (it is derived from glucose/O2 consumption). — [PMC3950304, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3950304/table/T1); [Levy & Calvert 2021 refer to the "oft-cited 20 W"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8106317)
- **Computation vs. communication in cortex:** Levy & Calvert (PNAS, 2021) estimate cortical *computation* at ~0.1 ATP-watts (≈1/200th of 20 W) and long-distance *communication* at ~3.5 W, i.e., communication costs ~35x more than computation; paper title: "Communication consumes 35 times more energy than computation in the human cortex, but both costs are needed to predict synapse number." [PR, 2021] — [Levy & Calvert 2021, PNAS (PMC8106317)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8106317). The 2020 bioRxiv preprint version gave ~0.17 W computation vs 4.6 W communication ("< 0.2 W" headline). [PP, 2020] — [bioRxiv 2020.04.23.057927](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.04.23.057927v1.full). A commentary notes they attribute ~27% of cortical power to synaptogenesis-related costs. — [Commentary, PMC8364152](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8364152)
- **Signaling dominates grey-matter energy; sparse codes forced by cost:** Attwell & Laughlin's bottom-up budget concludes a major fraction of grey-matter energy goes to signaling; one extra action potential per cortical neuron per second raises O2 consumption by ~145 mL/100 g grey matter/h; total budget ~40 µmol ATP/g/min (within the measured rodent range of 33–50); and the estimates "predict the use of distributed codes, with ≤15% of neurons simultaneously active." [PR, 2001 — older] — [Attwell & Laughlin 2001, J Cereb Blood Flow Metab](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1097/00004647-200110000-00001)
- **Lennie's tighter bound:** "The cost of a single spike is high, and this severely limits, possibly to fewer than 1%, the number of neurons that can be substantially active concurrently." [PR, 2003 — older] — [Lennie 2003, Current Biology (author PDF)](https://www2.bcs.rochester.edu/sites/plennie/pdfs/Lennie03a.pdf)
- **Revision of spike cost:** Howarth, Gleeson & Attwell (2012) updated these budgets and concluded action potentials in mammalian neurons are more energy-efficient than earlier models assumed (earlier models overestimated AP cost), so the <1% bound should be read as an upper-cost scenario. [PR, 2012] — [Howarth et al. 2012 (PMC3390818)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3390818)
- **Measured sparse firing:** Barth & Poulet's review of sensory cortex finds output highest in granular/infragranular layers; supragranular (L2/3) neurons have dense subthreshold activity but rare spikes, with some cells silent; caveat: classic extracellular recordings are biased toward the most active cells, so true sparseness is likely higher than older data suggested. [PR, 2012] — [Barth & Poulet 2012, Trends Neurosci (doi:10.1016/j.tins.2012.03.008)](https://www.mdc-berlin.de/research/publications/experimental-evidence-sparse-firing-neocortex)
- **Chip-side data-movement cost:** Horowitz's ISSCC 2014 figures (45 nm), as reproduced by co-authors and Sze: 32-bit DRAM read ≈ 640 pJ vs. 32-bit integer add ≈ 0.1 pJ (≈6,400x); 16-bit add ≈ 0.18 pJ, 64-bit add ≈ 5 pJ. [PR conference keynote, 2014 — older] — [Horowitz ISSCC 2014 slides/paper](https://www.gwern.net/doc/cs/hardware/2014-horowitz-2.pdf); [Pedram, Richardson, Galal, Kvatinsky, Horowitz "Dark Memory and Accelerator-Rich System Optimization" (table)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.04183). Horowitz's later talk: "Current DRAMs are about 1 nJ per 32-bit word" (~30 pJ/bit). — [Horowitz seminar slides, EPFL 2016](https://si2.epfl.ch/~demichel/si.epfl.ch/files/content/sites/si/files/shared/Seminar_Horowitz161209.pdf)
- **Why activation sparsity helps on-device:** TEAL's wall-clock speedup comes precisely from not moving weight channels for zeroed activations onto registers during memory-bound decoding. [PR, ICLR 2025] — [TEAL, arXiv 2408.14690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14690)

### Inferences
- The brain's efficiency is less about cheap arithmetic and more about (a) not moving data (weights live in synapses, computation happens at the synapse/dendrite), (b) event-driven sparse activity (only a few % of units signal at a time), and (c) low-precision, slow (≤~100 Hz, typically <1–10 Hz average) analog signalling. Levy & Calvert's 35:1 communication:computation ratio mirrors the DRAM-vs-ALU gap on chips.
- Back-of-envelope (my calculation, not from a source): a 1.2B-parameter model at 4-bit holds ~0.6 GB ≈ 4.8×10^9 bits; reading all weights once per generated token at Horowitz's ~30 pJ/bit costs ~0.14 J/token, i.e., ~3 W at 20 tokens/s just for DRAM traffic. Mobile LPDDR is likely cheaper per bit than this figure, but the order of magnitude explains why decode on phones is memory-bandwidth/energy bound and why "skip the weights you don't need" (activation sparsity, MoE) is the most transferable brain principle for a local companion.
- Brain "~20 W" includes housekeeping and maintenance; the part doing "computation" per Levy & Calvert is ~0.1 W. Comparisons of "brain 20 W vs GPU 700 W" conflate very different tasks and should be used only rhetorically.

### Gaps
- Total synapse count (commonly quoted as ~10^14–10^15) and per-synaptic-event energy (often quoted in the fJ range) were not verified by a primary source in this session.
- Energy per action potential in joules (Attwell & Laughlin / Howarth exact ATP-per-spike figures) not extracted from full text here.
- No primary source found for a modern (2024–2026) whole-brain wattage measurement; the 20 W figure is conventional.

## 2. Sparse activation and sparse coding: cortex evidence; AI analogues (activation sparsity, k-WTA, Numenta) and measured speedups

### Takeaway
Cortex uses sparse population codes (see Q1). In AI, sparsity has become one of the few brain principles with *real, measured* wall-clock gains: trained transformers are naturally activation-sparse (≈3–6% nonzero MLP activations with ReLU), and systems exploiting this achieve ~1.5–1.8x decode speedups training-free (TEAL) and up to ~25x vs. llama.cpp in memory-offload regimes on phones (PowerInfer-2). Mixture-of-experts (conditional computation) is the mainstream form. Unstructured weight/activation sparsity, however, needs ~90% sparsity to break even on commodity CPUs, and Numenta's large speedup claims are vendor-reported.

### Cited Findings
- **Classic sparse coding:** Olshausen & Field showed that learning a sparse code for natural images yields V1-like localized, oriented receptive fields. [PR, 1996 — older] [PK] — [Olshausen & Field 1996, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/381607a0)
- **Emergent activation sparsity in transformers ("lazy neuron"):** After ReLU, only ~3% of MLP activation entries are nonzero in T5-Base and ~6.3% in ViT-B/16; the phenomenon is prevalent across NLP and vision tasks, on train and eval data, and implies large possible FLOP reductions. [PR, ICLR 2023; arXiv Oct 2022] — [Li et al., "The Lazy Neuron Phenomenon", arXiv 2210.06313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06313)
- **Training-free activation sparsity at LLM scale (TEAL):** 40–50% model-wide sparsity with minimal degradation on Llama-2, Llama-3, Mistral (7B–70B); wall-clock single-batch decode speedups up to 1.53x (40%) and 1.8x (50%); compatible with weight quantization. [PR, ICLR 2025 spotlight; v3 Feb 2025] — [TEAL arXiv 2408.14690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14690); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/f3f2ff9579ba6deeb89caa2fe1f0b99c-Abstract-Conference.html); [code](https://github.com/FasterDecoding/TEAL)
- **Hot/cold neuron exploitation:** PowerInfer observed a small subset of "hot" neurons accounts for most activations (>80%) and split hot neurons to GPU, cold to CPU. [PP→PR (SOSP 2024), Dec 2023] — [PowerInfer arXiv 2312.12456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12456)
- **On a smartphone:** PowerInfer-2 served a 47B LLM on a phone at 11.68 tokens/s; up to 27.8x faster than state-of-the-art frameworks; average 3.94x (up to 4.38x) vs. Apple's "LLM in a Flash" and 25.4x (up to 29.2x) vs. llama.cpp; dense-activation neuron clusters run on the NPU, sparse ones on CPU. [PP, 2024] — [PowerInfer-2 arXiv 2406.06282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06282)
- **k-WTA / Numenta sparse networks:** Ahmad & Scheinkman showed networks with sparse weights + k-winners-take-all activations are more robust to noise with comparable accuracy (MNIST, Google Speech Commands). [PP, 2019 — older] [PK] — ["How Can We Be So Dense?" arXiv 1903.11257](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11257)
- **Numenta CPU inference claims:** Jan 2023 Numenta–Intel announcement: 123x BERT inference throughput vs. AMD Milan on short sequences; a later Intel customer story says 70x, of which ~3x comes from moving to 4th-Gen Xeon and ~19x from Numenta software; 20x throughput for 512-token sequences on Xeon Max; NuPIC on 5th-Gen Xeon >2x a batched NVIDIA GPU at batch size 1. Numenta's own HotChips 2023 slides note CPUs need ~90% sparsity (AVX-512/MKL) just to break even with dense matmul. [CO, 2023–2025; no independent replication found] — [Intel customer story](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/customer-spotlight/stories/numenta-transformer-networks-customer-story.html); [BusinessWire Jan 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230110005274/en); [ServeTheHome on Xeon Max / HotChips](https://www.servethehome.com/numenta-has-the-secret-to-ai-inference-on-cpus-like-the-intel-xeon-max/)
- **Spiking LLMs (SpikingBrain, CAS, Sept 2025):** SpikingBrain-7B (linear attention) and SpikingBrain-76B (hybrid-linear MoE) use adaptive-threshold spike coding giving ~69.15% activation sparsity; claims >100x faster time-to-first-token at 4M-token context for 7B, 15.39x speedup for a compressed 1B model on a CPU mobile framework at 256k length; ~150B tokens of continual pre-training to roughly match open transformer baselines; trained on MetaX C550 GPUs (23.4% MFU). [PP Sept 2025; listed as TMLR 2026 by PolyU] — [SpikingBrain arXiv 2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276); [PolyU listing](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/spikingbrain-spiking-brain-inspired-large-models/)
- **Neuromorphic hardware for LLMs:** A 370M-parameter MatMul-free LM run fully on Intel Loihi 2 (fixed-point) reports, in preliminary estimates, up to 3x higher throughput with 2x less energy vs. transformer LLMs on an edge GPU. [PP / ICLR 2025 workshop, Mar 2025] — [Abreu et al. arXiv 2503.18002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18002). The earlier MatMul-free LM paper claimed ≥4x throughput and ~10x energy efficiency vs comparable transformers on embedded GPUs (different setup). [PP, 2024] — [Zhu et al. arXiv 2406.02528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02528)
- **MoE as conditional computation in shipping on-device models:** LFM2-8B-A1B has 8.3B total / ~1.5B active parameters and Liquid AI claims 3–4B-class quality. [CO/PP, Nov 2025] — [LFM2 Technical Report arXiv 2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)

### Inferences
- Activation sparsity is the brain principle with the clearest, independently reproducible on-device payoff, because it directly attacks the memory-movement cost (Q1). The gains are 1.5–2x for training-free methods on dense-activation (SwiGLU) models, and much larger only when (a) models are ReLU-ified / trained for sparsity and (b) the model does not fit in fast memory (offloading regimes, e.g., PowerInfer-2 vs llama.cpp).
- SpikingBrain's headline 100x TTFT gain at 4M tokens is mostly attributable to linear attention (sub-quadratic sequence mixing), not to spikes per se; spike sparsity mainly pays off on event-driven hardware, which phones/laptops do not have.
- Numenta's 70–123x numbers mix hardware generation changes with software and use favorable baselines (AMD Milan CPU); treat as marketing until independently replicated.

### Gaps
- No independent, apples-to-apples benchmark of spiking LLMs vs. same-size transformers on standard tasks (MMLU etc.) on commodity mobile hardware was found.
- Energy (J/token), as opposed to speed, is rarely reported for activation-sparsity methods on phones.

## 3. Local / Hebbian learning and alternatives to backprop (forward-forward, equilibrium propagation, target propagation, feedback alignment): do any match backprop at scale?

### Takeaway
No. As of late 2026, no purely local learning rule has trained a model at modern LLM or ImageNet-224 scale to backprop-level accuracy. The best results: equilibrium propagation variants match backprop on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet 32×32 with small convnets; feedback-alignment variants that *learn* their feedback weights (weight mirror, Kolen-Pollack, product FA) approach backprop on ImageNet ResNets but are not truly local; forward-forward reached only ~26% top-1 on ImageNet (2025); Hebbian SoftHebb ~27% on ImageNet. For on-device learning, these remain research curiosities, not practical alternatives.

### Cited Findings
- **Original scaling negative result:** Bartunov et al. found biologically-motivated algorithms (target propagation, feedback alignment variants) fall far short of backprop on ImageNet. [PR, NeurIPS 2018 — older] [PK] — [Bartunov et al. arXiv 1807.04587](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04587)
- **Feedback alignment (FA):** Random fixed feedback weights can support learning. [PR, 2016 — older] [PK] — [Lillicrap et al. 2016, Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms13276). Benchmark (BioTorch): ImageNet ResNet-18 top-1 error 30.39% for BP vs 85.25% for FA, 34.97% for sign-symmetric uSF. [PP, 2021] — [Sanfiz & Akrout, arXiv 2108.13446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13446)
- **Learned-feedback variants nearly close the gap:** Weight Mirror and Kolen-Pollack reach ResNet-50 ImageNet top-1 error 23.4% / 23.9% vs. 22.9% BP; Product Feedback Alignment 68.46% (PFA) / 69.30% (PFA-o) top-1 on ResNet-18 vs 69.69% BP. (Numbers as reported in a 2026 review; originals: Akrout et al. 2019 [PK].) — [Review arXiv 2608.19995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19995); [Akrout et al. 2019 "Deep learning without weight transport", arXiv 1904.05391](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05391)
- **Direct feedback alignment on modern architectures:** DFA was shown to train transformers, NeRF, recommender systems etc. but with a performance gap on language modeling. [PR, NeurIPS 2020 — older] [PK] — [Launay et al. arXiv 2006.12878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12878)
- **Forward-Forward:** Hinton proposed replacing forward+backward passes with two forward passes (positive/negative data) and layer-local "goodness" objectives. [PP, Dec 2022] [PK] — [Hinton, arXiv 2212.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345). First claimed FF result on ImageNet (Adaptive Spatial Goodness Encoding, Sept 2025, rev. Jan 2026): 26.21% top-1 / 47.49% top-5 — far below backprop; authors note plain FF becomes impractical on ImageNet due to channel-dimension blow-up. [PP] — [ASGE, arXiv 2509.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12394)
- **Forward gradients with local losses:** Ren, Kornblith, Liao & Hinton scaled forward-gradient learning with many local losses to ImageNet, narrowing but not closing the gap to backprop. [PR, ICLR 2023] [PK for characterization] — [arXiv 2210.03310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03310)
- **Hebbian (SoftHebb):** Trains deep nets with no feedback/target/error signals; 99.4% MNIST, 80.3% CIFAR-10, 76.2% STL-10, 27.3% ImageNet (up to 5 hidden layers + linear classifier); a backprop-trained version of the same architecture reached 83.97% on CIFAR-10. [PR, ICLR 2023 oral] — [Journé et al. arXiv 2209.11883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11883); [ICLR 2023](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12759). A 2025 follow-up reports 81.11% CIFAR-10 and finds SoftHebb sensitive to architecture. [PP] — [arXiv 2510.14810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14810)
- **Equilibrium propagation (EP):** Introduced by Scellier & Bengio. [PR, 2017 — older] [PK] — [arXiv 1602.05179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05179). Symmetric nudging cut CIFAR-10 test error from 86.64% (standard EP) to 11.68%. [PR, 2021] — [Laborieux et al. 2021, Frontiers in Neuroscience](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2021.633674/pdf). Holomorphic EP computes exact gradients and matches BP on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet 32×32 with a five-layer VGG-style CNN. [PR, NeurIPS 2022] — [Laborieux & Zenke arXiv 2209.00530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530). ICLR 2024 follow-up scales hEP to ImageNet 32×32 without strict weight symmetry. [PR, ICLR 2024] — [arXiv 2309.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02214)
- **Predictive coding as a backprop alternative:** μPC (NeurIPS 2025) trains residual networks up to 128 layers with PC on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 with competitive performance; standard PC scaled poorly because the inference landscape becomes ill-conditioned with width/depth; experiments limited to fully connected ResNets on simple tasks. [PR, NeurIPS 2025] — [Innocenti, Achour, Buckley, NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7ea5ad70c984262c064ee9859f7e5f56-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2505.13124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13124)

### Inferences
- The closest-to-backprop methods (learned feedback, hEP, μPC) mostly work by *approximating backprop's gradient* more faithfully; their benefit is biological plausibility or analog/neuromorphic-hardware compatibility, not better sample- or energy-efficiency on GPUs/CPUs today.
- For a phone/laptop companion, on-device learning should use backprop-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, sparse updates) during idle/charging time rather than local learning rules; local rules become attractive only on analog/in-memory or neuromorphic chips that are not in consumer devices.
- Main structural advantage of local rules (no need to store activations for a backward pass → lower memory) is real in principle but has not been demonstrated to yield better accuracy-per-joule at useful scale.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 standardized benchmark comparing FF, FA, EP, PC, target propagation under one ImageNet-224 protocol was found.
- Target propagation (difference TP and successors) 2024–2026 large-scale results were not located in this session.
- No reports of any local rule training a language model beyond toy scale were found.

## 4. Predictive coding and the free-energy principle / active inference (Friston; VERSES Genius): evidence and practical AI results

### Takeaway
Neuroscience support for predictive coding is real but mixed (early evidence weak, newer studies stronger, key signatures like repetition suppression ambiguous); the free-energy principle is broad enough that critics call it unfalsifiable. As an ML method, PC now scales to 100+ layer networks on small image tasks (μPC, NeurIPS 2025) but has no LLM-scale results. Active inference's flagship practical claims come from VERSES: AXIOM (May 2025 preprint) beats DreamerV3 on VERSES' Gameworld 10k with ~0.95M vs 420M params and far less compute, and Genius beat LLMs at Mastermind — all company claims with one paid validation report that is more nuanced than the press release.

### Cited Findings
- **Free-energy principle (theory):** Friston proposed that brains minimize variational free energy (prediction error / surprise bound) as a unified account of perception, action, learning. [PR, 2010 — older] [PK] — [Friston 2010, Nat Rev Neurosci](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2787)
- **Evidence review:** Predictive processing has been criticized for lacking empirical support, with a recent surge of human and animal neurophysiology seeking to fill the gap; the review evaluates how well data support core claims. [PR, 2020] — [Walsh et al. 2020, Ann NY Acad Sci (PMC7187369)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187369)
- **Weak historical evidence, stronger recent:** Many historical studies only weakly showed predictive coding; modern approaches yield stronger support; field siloed in human neuroimaging; repetition suppression hard to attribute to prediction error. [Journalism summarizing PR review] — [Simons Foundation, "The Challenges of Proving Predictive Coding"](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/?p=13027)
- **Theory review:** Predictive coding mathematics, candidate microcircuits, and links to backprop. [PP, 2021] — [Millidge, Seth, Buckley, arXiv 2107.12979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12979)
- **Critique (2025):** Argues the Bayesian-brain/FEP framework suffers from unfalsifiability, biological implausibility, inconsistent empirical support, and that FEP shifts interpretive ground across levels to avoid refutation. (Caveat: the journal venue appears unusual for the subject; verify.) — [Springer article s00421-025-05855-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-025-05855-6)
- **PC ↔ backprop:** PC networks can approximate backprop on arbitrary computation graphs. [PP 2020] [PK] — [Millidge, Tschantz, Buckley arXiv 2006.04182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04182). "Prospective configuration" (infer activity first, then update weights) argued to learn faster than backprop in some biologically relevant settings (continual/online, few-shot). [PR, Nature Neuroscience 2024] [PK] — [Song et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01514-1)
- **PC tooling/benchmarks:** PCX JAX library + standardized benchmark; authors test larger architectures than usual and report new SOTA *for PC* across their tasks. [PR, ICLR 2025] — [Pinchetti et al. ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/581df42e8ebbeeac39aeda03519b7c0e-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **PC depth scaling:** μPC trains 128-layer ResNets (MNIST/F-MNIST/CIFAR-10) — see Q3. [PR, NeurIPS 2025] — [μPC](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7ea5ad70c984262c064ee9859f7e5f56-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **AXIOM (VERSES, active inference):** Object-centric generative model; scenes as compositions of objects with piecewise-linear dynamics; no neural networks, backprop or gradient descent (per VERSES). [PP, May 2025] — [AXIOM arXiv 2505.24784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24784). VERSES claims vs DreamerV3 on Gameworld 10k (10 arcade-style games, 10k interaction steps): up to 60% better, 97% more efficient, 39x faster learning, 12x cheaper to run, 400x smaller (0.95M vs 420M parameters). [CO, June 2, 2025] — [VERSES press release](https://www.verses.ai/news/verses-digital-brain-beats-googles-top-ai-at-gameworld-10k-atari-challenge)
- **Paid validation is more nuanced:** Soothsayer Analytics' report confirms AXIOM outperformed DreamerV3 on the 1,000-step moving average and had much lower training time in nearly all environments, but DreamerV3 outperformed AXIOM in "Cross," and gains in "Drive" and "Impact" were within 1%. [Third-party, commissioned] — [Soothsayer validation report (PDF)](https://www.verses.ai/hubfs/images/blog/2025-05%20Gameworld/Soothsayer%20VERSES%20Axiom%20Technical%20Validation%20Report.pdf)
- **Genius / Mastermind:** VERSES reports Genius solved 100/100 Mastermind games vs 45% for DeepSeek R1, using ~5 minutes total compute vs 26 hours, and estimated cost ~$0.05 vs $38.94; earlier claimed to beat OpenAI o1-preview. Genius launched commercially April 2025; Aug 2025 claim of outperforming others on Meta's Habitat benchmark without pre-training. [CO, 2025] — [VERSES research summary](https://www.verses.ai/research-summary); [affiliated commentary on o1 comparison](https://deniseholt.substack.com/p/verses-ai-crushes-openai-o1-in-head)
- **Commercial status:** VERSES' Dec 2025 newsletter says it cut internal costs and narrowed its product to target data scientists/developers, focusing on revenue in 2026. [CO] — [VERSES Dec 2025 newsletter](https://www.verses.ai/news/verses-monthly-newsletter-december-2025)

### Inferences
- Active inference/Bayesian object-centric models show genuine sample-efficiency advantages on *structured, low-dimensional* environments (simple physics games, puzzles like Mastermind) where the right generative model can be hand-specified; Mastermind is a constraint-satisfaction problem where comparing against general LLMs is an uneven test.
- Gameworld 10k appears to be a benchmark constructed by the AXIOM authors themselves (my reading; verify in the paper), which raises benchmark-selection concerns.
- For a personal companion, active inference is most plausibly useful as a *planning/belief-tracking layer* (e.g., modeling user state, deciding when to ask vs. act) on top of a language model — not as a replacement for the language model. No evidence of language-modeling competitiveness was found.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-VERSES-commissioned) replication of AXIOM or Genius results found.
- No predictive-coding-trained language model with benchmark results found.
- Direct quantitative neuroscience evidence (e.g., Allen Institute OpenScope predictive-processing results, 2024–2026) not retrieved.

## 5. Continual learning without catastrophic forgetting: synaptic consolidation (EWC, SI), replay, complementary learning systems (hippocampus + neocortex, sleep). Current best AI methods

### Takeaway
Complementary learning systems (CLS) theory — fast hippocampal episodic storage plus slow neocortical consolidation via replay, much of it during sleep — is the best-supported brain blueprint and maps directly onto practical AI: replay buffers, retrieval/external memory, and periodic consolidation. Regularization methods (EWC, SI) are useful but weak alone; replay remains the strongest general baseline. In 2025–2026 the most promising LLM-scale results come from *sparse* updates: Meta's Sparse Memory Finetuning cut forgetting from 89% (full FT) / 71% (LoRA) to 11% at equal new-knowledge acquisition, and Google's Nested Learning/HOPE introduces multi-timescale "continuum memory". A further, often overlooked problem is *loss of plasticity* (Dohare et al., Nature 2024).

### Cited Findings
- **CLS theory updated:** Intelligent agents need two learning systems: neocortex gradually builds structured knowledge; hippocampus rapidly stores specific experiences; replay of hippocampal memories supports consolidation and generalization; consolidation is complete when retrieval no longer needs the hippocampus; DeepMind's DQN experience replay cited as an AI instance. [PR, Trends Cogn Sci 2016 — older] — [Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016 (author PDF)](https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/KumaranHassabisMcClelland16FinalMS.pdf); original CLS: [McClelland, McNaughton, O'Reilly 1995, Psych Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419) [PK]
- **Synaptic consolidation in AI:** EWC (Kirkpatrick et al.) slows learning on weights important for old tasks (Fisher-information penalty), inspired by synaptic consolidation. [PR, PNAS 2017 — older] [PK] — [Kirkpatrick et al. 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1611835114). Synaptic Intelligence computes per-synapse importance online. [PR, ICML 2017 — older] [PK] — [Zenke, Poole, Ganguli arXiv 1703.04200](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04200)
- **Brain-inspired (generative) replay:** Replay of internal/hidden representations generated by the network's own generative model, without storing raw data, achieved strong class-incremental results. [PR, Nat Commun 2020] [PK] — [van de Ven, Siegelmann, Tolias 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17866-2)
- **Sleep-like replay:** Sleep Replay Consolidation (SRC) — an offline phase with local unsupervised Hebbian plasticity and noisy input (Heaviside activations) — reduces catastrophic forgetting in ANNs. [PR, Nat Commun Dec 2022] — [Tadros, Krishnan, Ramyaa, Bazhenov 2022 (PMC9755223)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9755223/)
- **Loss of plasticity:** Standard deep learning gradually loses the ability to learn in continual settings, eventually learning no better than a shallow network (shown on continual ImageNet and RL); in the preprint, binary classification accuracy dropped from 89% on early tasks to 77% by task 2,000. Fix: "continual backpropagation" reinitializes a tiny fraction of less-used units each step; L2 + weight perturbation also helps. [PR, Nature, Aug 2024] — [Dohare et al. 2024 (PMC11338828)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11338828/); [preprint arXiv 2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812v1)
- **Sparse Memory Finetuning (Meta FAIR + UC Berkeley, Oct 2025):** Updates only memory-layer slots that a batch activates disproportionately (TF-IDF vs. background corpus). Learning a TriviaQA fact stream, NaturalQuestions performance dropped 89% with full finetuning, 71% with LoRA, only 11% with SMF at the same new-knowledge acquisition. [PP, Oct 2025] — [Lin et al. arXiv 2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103)
- **SMF follow-ups:** KL-divergence slot scoring reveals a task-dependent stability–plasticity trade-off. [PP, Apr 2026] — [arXiv 2604.05248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05248). Independent re-implementation on Qwen-2.5-0.5B-Instruct (MedMCQA): SMF +2.5 pts vs LoRA +4.6 and full FT +5.4, but SMF keeps forgetting probes within ~1 pt of base. [PR, ICML 2026] — [ICML 2026 paper page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/69392)
- **Nested Learning / HOPE (Google, NeurIPS 2025):** Treats a model as nested optimization problems updating at different frequencies; "continuum memory system" (CMS) = spectrum of memory modules each updating at a specific rate; HOPE (a self-modifying Titans variant) reported lower perplexity and higher accuracy than modern recurrent models and standard transformers on language modeling/common-sense reasoning, and better needle-in-haystack results (blog-level summary). [PR (NeurIPS 2025) + CO blog, Nov 7 2025] — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/introducing-nested-learning-a-new-ml-paradigm-for-continual-learning/)
- **Neuromodulation for continual learning:** see Q7 ([review arXiv 2501.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06762)). Active dendrites for continual learning: see Q6.

### Inferences
- A practical CLS-style design for a local companion: frozen or slowly updated base LLM ("neocortex") + fast, append-only episodic store with retrieval ("hippocampus") + periodic offline consolidation ("sleep") while charging, using replay of stored episodes mixed with general data and sparse/low-rank updates (SMF-style memory layers or LoRA) to limit forgetting. Every component exists today; the brain-inspired part is the architecture, not exotic learning rules.
- The SMF independent replication shows the core trade-off: lower forgetting comes with smaller in-task gains; for a companion where preserving general competence matters more than maximal fact uptake, that trade is attractive.
- Loss of plasticity matters for an always-learning personal model; cheap mitigations (L2, small resets, shrink-and-perturb) should be part of any long-lived on-device fine-tuning loop.

### Gaps
- Scale of the SMF base model and exact benchmarks beyond TriviaQA/NQ not confirmed (full paper not fetched).
- HOPE's exact numbers/model sizes not verified beyond the blog.
- No head-to-head 2025–2026 benchmark found comparing EWC/SI vs replay vs SMF on LLM-scale personal-knowledge continual learning.

## 6. Dendritic computation (single neurons as multi-layer networks; Beniaguev et al. 2021) and active dendrites in AI

### Takeaway
A single detailed cortical L5 pyramidal neuron needs a 5–8-layer temporal convolutional network to emulate at millisecond precision, driven mainly by NMDA-dependent dendritic nonlinearities. AI work borrowing this (dendritic ANNs, Numenta active dendrites) shows better parameter efficiency, robustness and reduced forgetting on small benchmarks (MNIST-family, CIFAR-scale, permuted MNIST, Meta-World), but no large-scale or language results.

### Cited Findings
- **Neuron-as-deep-net:** Deep nets trained to reproduce detailed L5 pyramidal neuron I/O at ms resolution generalized well; removing NMDA receptors let a single-hidden-layer network suffice, so complexity stems mainly from NMDA dendritic nonlinearities. [PR, Neuron 2021] — [Beniaguev, Segev, London 2021 (HUJI record)](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/single-cortical-neurons-as-deep-artificial-neural-networks/); [code/data](https://github.com/SelfishGene/neuron_as_deep_net)
- **Size estimate:** Popular coverage reports 5–8 layers needed; the ELSC write-up phrases it as roughly 600–900 classical point neurons (same result, different units). — [Quanta Magazine, Sept 2021](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-computationally-complex-is-a-single-neuron-20210902/); [ELSC "In the Spotlight"](https://elsc.huji.ac.il/?p=18426)
- **Dendritic ANNs (Chavlis & Poirazi):** ANN with dendrite-like structured connectivity is more robust to overfitting and matches or beats standard nets on several image classification tasks with far fewer trainable parameters; most nodes respond to multiple classes (vs. class-specific nodes in classical ANNs). [PR, Nat Commun Jan 2025] — [Chavlis & Poirazi 2025, Nat Commun (doi 10.1038/s41467-025-56297-9)](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41467-025-56297-9); [preprint arXiv 2404.03708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03708); [FORTH press release](https://www.forth.gr/en/news/show/&tid=2788)
- **Numenta active dendrites:** Neurons with dendritic segments that receive a context signal gate which sub-network is active (combined with k-WTA sparsity); evaluated on multi-task RL (Meta-World) and continual learning (permuted MNIST, single head), reducing interference. [PR, Frontiers in Neurorobotics 2022] — [Iyer et al. 2022 "Avoiding Catastrophe"](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2022.846219/full); [code](https://www.github.com/numenta/htmpapers/blob/master/biorxiv/going_beyond_the_point_neuron)

### Inferences
- The practical AI translation of dendrites is essentially *context-gated, sparse sub-network selection* — conceptually close to mixture-of-experts routing and task-conditioned adapters, which already work at scale. Explicit dendritic neuron models have not been shown to beat MoE/adapters on real benchmarks.
- Beniaguev's result implies brain "neuron counts" understate compute capacity by 2–3 orders of magnitude when mapping to ANN units, which undercuts naive "brain = N parameters" comparisons.

### Gaps
- Exact accuracy tables for Numenta's permuted-MNIST / MT10 experiments and Chavlis & Poirazi parameter-reduction factors were not retrieved.
- No dendritic model evaluated on language modeling or LLM benchmarks found.

## 7. Neuromodulation (dopamine, acetylcholine) as learning-rate / attention control; AI analogues (meta-learning, neuromodulated plasticity)

### Takeaway
Neuromodulators act as global "third factors" gating when and how much synapses change (dopamine ≈ reward-prediction error; acetylcholine ≈ attention/learning-rate/uncertainty). The dopamine–TD-error correspondence is the single most successful neuroscience↔AI convergence (it underlies RL). Explicit neuromodulated plasticity in ANNs (differentiable plasticity, backpropamine, SpikePropamine) works on small meta-learning tasks; at scale its function is subsumed by learned gating, attention, adaptive optimizers and in-context learning.

### Cited Findings
- **Dopamine = reward prediction error:** Midbrain dopamine firing matches temporal-difference prediction error. [PR, Science 1997 — older] [PK] — [Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)
- **Neuromodulators as meta-parameters:** Doya proposed dopamine ↔ TD error, serotonin ↔ discount factor, noradrenaline ↔ exploration (inverse temperature), acetylcholine ↔ learning rate / memory update speed. [PR, Neural Networks 2002 — older] [PK] — [Doya 2002 "Metalearning and neuromodulation"](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00044-8)
- **Three-factor learning rules:** Hebbian eligibility traces + a global neuromodulatory third factor (often dopamine) form a widely used framework. [PR, 2016 — older] [PK] — [Frémaux & Gerstner 2016, Front Neural Circuits](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2015.00085)
- **Differentiable / neuromodulated plasticity:** Weights have a learned Hebbian plastic component trained by backprop (meta-learning); backpropamine adds a network-controlled neuromodulatory signal gating plasticity, improving on small RL/meta-learning and language-modeling tasks vs. non-plastic LSTMs. [PR, ICML 2018 / ICLR 2019 — older] [PK] — [Miconi et al. 2018, arXiv 1804.02464](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02464); [Miconi et al. "Backpropamine", arXiv 2002.10585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10585). Spiking variant: SpikePropamine argues most neuromodulatory signals beyond dopamine are excluded from AI learning algorithms. [PP, 2021] — [arXiv 2106.02681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02681)
- **2025 review for continual learning:** Covers dopamine, acetylcholine, serotonin, noradrenaline; argues multi-neuromodulator interactions and multiple spatio-temporal scales enrich learning, and surveys ways to approximate neuromodulated learning in ANNs (incl. Go/No-Go dopamine case study). [PP, Jan 2025] — [arXiv 2501.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06762)

### Inferences
- For a companion AI, the useful neuromodulation analogue is *gated consolidation*: only write to long-term memory / trigger fine-tuning when a salience signal (surprise, explicit user feedback, emotional importance) is high — an engineering policy rather than a new learning rule. This matches SMF-style selective updating (Q5).
- Evidence that explicit neuromodulated plasticity outperforms standard training on real (non-toy) benchmarks was not found.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 large-scale benchmark of neuromodulated-plasticity networks vs. transformers found.
- Quantitative acetylcholine-as-learning-rate neuroscience data not retrieved in this session.

## 8. Thousand Brains theory (Numenta / Thousand Brains Project, Monty): status and results

### Takeaway
The Thousand Brains Project (independent non-profit spun out of Numenta, Gates Foundation-funded, MIT-licensed code) has a working sensorimotor system, Monty, built from many cortical-column-like "learning modules" that learn object models in reference frames and vote. Its peer-reviewed 2026 paper (Neural Computation) shows strong *object and pose recognition* on 77 YCB objects (98.6%) with ~4M parameters and orders of magnitude fewer training FLOPs than ViTs, plus continual learning via Hebbian-like binding. It is explicitly a research platform (API v0) with no language capability; it does not yet bear on building a conversational companion.

### Cited Findings
- **Theory:** Cortical columns each learn complete models of objects using grid-cell-like reference frames; many columns vote. [PR, 2019 — older] [PK] — [Hawkins et al. 2019, Front Neural Circuits](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2018.00121/full)
- **Project launch & white paper:** "The Thousand Brains Project: A New Paradigm for Sensorimotor Intelligence" introduces Monty's design. [PP, Dec 2024] — [arXiv 2412.18354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18354); coverage — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/jeff-hawkins)
- **Monty results (peer-reviewed):** Leadholm, Clay, Knudstrup, Lee, Hawkins, "Thousand-Brains Systems: Sensorimotor Intelligence for Rapid, Robust Learning and Inference," Neural Computation 38(6):845–896, May 2026 (arXiv July 2025). Tests 3D object recognition and pose estimation on YCB; model-free and model-based movement policies; multi-module voting accelerates inference; associative Hebbian-like binding for continual learning; authors call Monty "nascent." [PR, 2026] — [DOI 10.1162/NECO.a.1508](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO.a.1508); [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04494)
- **Numbers:** Recognizes all 77 YCB objects at 98.6% accuracy including rotation; ~4M parameters after learning all of YCB; requires ~528,000,000x fewer training FLOPs than the pretrained ViT (the only baseline matching its accuracy) and ~34,000x fewer than a ViT trained from scratch (earlier version); better accuracy per inference FLOP than ViTs; with one epoch of data Monty ~50% vs ViT ~1% (explainer). ViT FLOPs estimated with calflops. [PR + project explainer] — [arXiv 2507.04494 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04494); [TBP plain-language explainer](https://thousandbrains.org/thousand-brain-systems-sensorimotor-intelligence-for-rapid-robust-learning-and-inference-a-plain-language-explainer/)
- **Status 2026:** Repo says Monty is a research project, not production-ready, API in major version 0; project is an independent non-profit partially funded by the Gates Foundation; 197 PRs merged in Q1 2026. — [GitHub tbp.monty](https://github.com/thousandbrainsproject/tbp.monty); [Q2 2026 roadmap post](https://thousandbrains.discourse.group/t/2026-04-q2-roadmap-and-q1-review/1066)

### Inferences
- The FLOP comparison is real but on a task tailored to Monty's strengths (embodied, multi-view, rotation-aware recognition of a small object set); the ViT baselines are not state-of-the-art 3D/pose methods. The 528-million-x figure mostly reflects ViT ImageNet pretraining cost.
- Relevance to a local companion: conceptually aligned (continual, sample-efficient, cheap), but there is no pathway yet to language, dialogue or abstract reasoning. Treat as a long-horizon watch item.

### Gaps
- No independent replication of Monty results; no comparison against modern 3D/pose-estimation specialist models found.
- No published Monty work on language or abstract concepts found.

## 9. Liquid neural networks / CfC (MIT, Liquid AI) and Liquid AI's LFM models: benchmarks vs. same-size transformers

### Takeaway
The original "liquid" networks (LTC 2021, CfC 2022, NCPs 2020) are small continuous-time RNNs inspired by C. elegans neurons, strong on time-series/control with tiny parameter counts. Liquid AI's commercial LFM2/LFM2.5 models, however, are architecturally a hybrid of gated short convolutions (10 blocks) and grouped-query attention (6 blocks) found by hardware-in-the-loop search — efficient, but not LTC/CfC networks. They are genuinely competitive on-device: LFM2-1.2B ≈ Qwen3-1.7B on benchmarks with up to ~2x faster CPU prefill/decode (company-reported), and LFM2.5-1.2B hits ~116 tok/s decode on a laptop CPU. But they are trained on 10–28T tokens — no data-efficiency advantage.

### Cited Findings
- **LTC / NCP origins:** Liquid time-constant networks: neurons with input-dependent time constants (ODE-based). [PR, AAAI 2021] [PK] — [Hasani et al. arXiv 2006.04439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04439). Neural circuit policies: 19-neuron C. elegans-inspired controller for lane keeping. [PR, Nature MI 2020] [PK] — [Lechner et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-020-00237-3)
- **CfC:** Closed-form approximation of LTC dynamics; at least one order of magnitude faster than ODE-based counterparts with equal modeling ability; outperformed advanced recurrent models on diverse time-series tasks incl. long-term dependencies and irregular sampling. [PR, Nature Machine Intelligence 4:992–1003, Nov 2022] — [Hasani et al. arXiv 2106.13898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13898); [NMI paper PDF](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/502996267/s42256_022_00556_7.pdf); [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2022/solving-brain-dynamics-gives-rise-flexible-machine-learning-models-1115)
- **LFM2 architecture:** Hybrid backbone with majority gated short-convolution blocks and minority GQA blocks: 16 blocks = 10 double-gated short-conv + 6 GQA; short depthwise 1D convs with input-dependent gates, chosen partly for CPU cache behavior; family 350M–8.3B (dense 350M, 700M, 1.2B, 2.6B; MoE 8.3B total / 1.5B active); pre-trained on 10–12T tokens. [PP tech report Nov 28, 2025 + CO] — [LFM2 Technical Report arXiv 2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404); [LFM2-1.2B model card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2-1.2B); [Liquid AI LFM2 blog (July 2025)](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/liquid-foundation-models-v2-our-second-series-of-generative-ai-models); [third-party write-up of the hardware-in-the-loop search](https://marcodsn.me/blog/lfm2-architecture-search)
- **LFM2 accuracy vs. same-size transformers (model card):** LFM2-1.2B vs Qwen3-1.7B: MMLU 55.23 vs 59.11; GSM8K 58.3 vs 51.4; IFEval 74.89 vs 73.98. Liquid also claims LFM2-700M beats Gemma 3 1B IT and LFM2-350M is competitive with Qwen3-0.6B and Llama 3.2 1B Instruct. [CO] — [LFM2-1.2B model card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2-1.2B/blob/26bb21ffbfe2d9527e007758ddddb3700955d2af/README.md); [Liquid AI blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/liquid-foundation-models-v2-our-second-series-of-generative-ai-models)
- **LFM2 speed:** Up to 2x faster CPU prefill and decode than similarly sized models (report); on a Samsung Galaxy S25 (llama.cpp, Q4_0, batch 1), LFM2-350M ~2–3x prefill and ~1.5–2.0x decode throughput vs Granite-4.0-350M; laptop tests on AMD Ryzen HX 370. [PP/CO] — [LFM2 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)
- **LFM2.5 (2026):** Jan 2026 LFM2.5-1.2B family (Base/Instruct/Japanese/VL/audio), open weights; LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct: 2,975 prefill / 116 decode tok/s on AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 CPU (llama.cpp Q4_0). LFM2.5-350M trained on 28T tokens (up from 10T). LFM2.5-VL-3B (Aug 2026): 228 tok/s on M5 Max, ~3 GB memory, 20 tok/s on Galaxy S26 Ultra. LFM2.5-24B-A2B (Oct 2026): ~2B active params, 184 tok/s on M5 Max, competitive with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on 5 of 8 agentic/tool-use benchmarks. [CO] — [LFM2.5 launch blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/introducing-lfm2-5-the-next-generation-of-on-device-ai); [LFM2.5-350M blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-350m-no-size-left-behind); [LFM2.5-VL-3B (Hugging Face blog)](https://huggingface.co/blog/LiquidAI/lfm2-5-vl-3b); [LFM2.5-24B-A2B blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-24b-a2b); [third-party setup guide w/ speeds](https://insiderllm.com/guides/liquidai-lfm2-local-setup-guide/)

### Inferences
- "Liquid" in LFM2 is primarily lineage/branding: the shipping architecture is a conv+attention hybrid in the same family as other efficient hybrids (state-space/convolution + some attention). Its wins over transformers are modest (≈ one size class of quality-per-parameter, ~1.5–2x CPU speed) and come from systems-aware architecture search, not from continuous-time neuron dynamics.
- LFM2/LFM2.5 are among the strongest practical candidates for a local companion backbone today, but they are as data-hungry as other LLMs (10–28T tokens), so they do not demonstrate brain-like data efficiency.
- CfC/LTC remain attractive for small streaming sensor/time-series sub-modules (e.g., activity or physiological signal modeling on a phone) rather than language.

### Gaps
- No independent third-party benchmark (e.g., from Artificial Analysis or academic evals) of LFM2/LFM2.5 vs Qwen3/Gemma at matched size and quantization was found; numbers are Liquid-reported.
- Energy (J/token) measurements on phones for LFM2 vs transformers not found.
- No published evidence that LTC/CfC layers are used inside LFM2 was found (the report describes gated convolutions + GQA).

## 10. Honest assessment: which brain principles have translated into practical wins, which remain speculative

### Takeaway
Clear practical wins: (1) sparsity/conditional computation (activation sparsity, MoE) with measured 1.5–2x+ speedups and much more in offload regimes; (2) CLS-style memory (replay, external episodic memory/retrieval, sparse selective consolidation) for continual learning; (3) dopamine/TD learning as the basis of RL. Partial/narrow wins: liquid/CfC networks for small time-series tasks; efficient recurrent/conv hybrids (LFM2) whose brain link is loose; active inference and Monty in narrow sensorimotor/puzzle domains (company or single-group results). Still speculative at useful scale: local/Hebbian learning replacing backprop, predictive coding for large models, spiking LLMs on commodity hardware, dendritic neurons, explicit neuromodulated plasticity, and the free-energy principle as an engineering recipe. Data efficiency remains the biggest unsolved gap: BabyLM-style models trained on ≤100M words show some gains but brain-like data efficiency has not been achieved.

### Cited Findings
- **Sparsity wins are measured:** TEAL 1.53–1.8x decode speedups at 40–50% sparsity on 7B–70B LLMs [PR, ICLR 2025] — [arXiv 2408.14690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14690); PowerInfer-2 47B on a phone at 11.68 tok/s, up to 29.2x vs llama.cpp [PP] — [arXiv 2406.06282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06282); MoE in on-device models (LFM2-8B-A1B, 1.5B active) [CO/PP] — [arXiv 2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)
- **Continual learning wins are emerging at LLM scale:** SMF forgetting 11% vs 71% (LoRA) vs 89% (full FT) [PP] — [arXiv 2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103); independent confirmation of low forgetting with smaller gains [PR, ICML 2026] — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/69392); CLS theory as blueprint [PR] — [Kumaran et al. 2016](https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/KumaranHassabisMcClelland16FinalMS.pdf)
- **Local learning has not matched backprop at scale:** FF 26.21% ImageNet top-1 [PP, 2025] — [arXiv 2509.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12394); SoftHebb 27.3% ImageNet [PR] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11883); hEP matches BP only up to ImageNet 32×32 [PR] — [arXiv 2209.00530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530); μPC limited to simple tasks [PR] — [NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7ea5ad70c984262c064ee9859f7e5f56-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Active inference / Thousand Brains are narrow but real research results:** AXIOM (company preprint, commissioned validation shows mixed per-game results) — [Soothsayer report](https://www.verses.ai/hubfs/images/blog/2025-05%20Gameworld/Soothsayer%20VERSES%20Axiom%20Technical%20Validation%20Report.pdf); Monty peer-reviewed but research-stage — [Neural Computation 2026](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO.a.1508); [tbp.monty repo](https://github.com/thousandbrainsproject/tbp.monty)
- **Spiking/neuromorphic LLMs are preliminary:** Loihi 2 MatMul-free 370M LM: up to 3x throughput, 2x less energy vs edge GPU, preliminary estimates [PP] — [arXiv 2503.18002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18002); SpikingBrain claims rely heavily on linear attention and non-NVIDIA hardware [PP] — [arXiv 2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276)
- **Data efficiency (BabyLM):** The BabyLM shared task caps training at ≤100M words (10M for Strict-Small); the 2025 (3rd) edition added an Interaction track and counts repeated passes toward exposure budgets. [PR workshop, Nov 2025] — [Findings of the 3rd BabyLM Challenge (ACL Anthology)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28); [2025 call, arXiv 2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08165). Organizers report that 2023 winners (LTG-BERT-based) outperformed models trained on trillions of words on the challenge's evaluation suite (search-extract claim; tables not inspected). — [Findings of the 2nd BabyLM Challenge, arXiv 2412.05149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05149)
- **Even on-device "brain-inspired" LLMs are data-hungry:** LFM2 10–12T tokens; LFM2.5-350M 28T tokens. [CO/PP] — [LFM2 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404); [LFM2.5-350M blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-350m-no-size-left-behind)
- **Loss of plasticity is a real obstacle for lifelong learners** [PR, Nature 2024] — [Dohare et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11338828/)

### Inferences
- Ranking of brain principles by practical value for a private on-device companion (my synthesis):
  1. **Sparse / conditional computation** (activation sparsity, MoE, skip-weight-loading) — proven, directly reduces memory traffic (the dominant energy cost per Q1). Use now.
  2. **Complementary learning systems** (frozen/slow base model + fast episodic memory + retrieval + gated, sparse offline consolidation with replay during charging "sleep") — all parts exist; SMF/LoRA + replay are the practical tools. Use now.
  3. **Neuromodulation as gating policy** (salience-gated memory writes / updates) — cheap engineering heuristic; use now.
  4. **Efficient sequence mixers inspired by continuous-time dynamics** (conv/SSM/linear-attention hybrids like LFM2; CfC for sensor streams) — practical, modest gains (~1.5–2x CPU speed, ~1 size-class quality/param), loosely brain-inspired.
  5. **Active inference / Thousand Brains / object-centric world models** — promising for narrow planning or sensorimotor sub-modules; not for language; mostly single-group or company evidence.
  6. **Local learning rules, predictive coding training, spiking networks, dendritic neurons** — scientifically interesting, not yet competitive at useful scale on phone/laptop hardware; monitor, don't build on.
- The brain's biggest advantages (≈0.1 W of "computation", lifelong learning from ~10^8 words, no catastrophic forgetting) arise from the *co-design* of hardware (in-memory analog synapses, event-driven spikes) and algorithms. On commodity phones/laptops, only the algorithm-level principles that reduce memory traffic and protect old knowledge transfer today; the hardware-level principles (in-memory compute, spikes) require neuromorphic/analog chips that are not yet in consumer devices.
- Data efficiency is the least-solved dimension: none of the methods above have demonstrated child-like language learning from ~100M words at competitive quality; a local companion should therefore start from a pretrained small LLM and use brain-inspired principles for *adaptation and memory*, not for learning language from scratch.

### Gaps
- No rigorous, independent energy-per-token comparison across brain-inspired vs standard small LLMs on the same phone hardware was found.
- BabyLM 2025 winning scores and how they compare to frontier models were not retrieved.
- Evidence on how much of SpikingBrain's efficiency is attributable to spiking vs linear attention vs MoE is not disentangled in available summaries.
- Full texts of several 2025–2026 arXiv papers (LFM2, AXIOM, SMF, Monty) could not be fetched due to network restrictions; numbers come from search extracts, model cards and secondary pages and should be spot-checked against the PDFs.
