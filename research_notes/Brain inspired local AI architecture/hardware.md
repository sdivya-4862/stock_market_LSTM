# Brain-Inspired and Non-Conventional AI Hardware vs. Today's Consumer Devices (status as of Nov 2026)

Notes on scope and method. These notes cover neuromorphic, analog/in-memory, photonic and biological computing, plus the memory-bandwidth limits of current phones and laptops. The end use is judging whether a private AI companion could run offline on a phone or laptop. Each finding is tagged **[MEASURED]** (a hardware measurement in a paper or benchmark), **[CLAIM]** (a vendor or marketing figure, or a projection) or **[SIM]** (a simulation or estimate), wherever the tag can be told apart. Many primary hosts (arxiv.org, nature.com, research.ibm.com, modha.org, tomshardware.com, 9to5mac.com, physicsworld.com) were blocked in this research session. So several numbers come from search-engine excerpts of those primary pages, not from full-text reads, and they are flagged as such. Dates are publication or announcement dates. Results from 2023–2024 are marked **(older)**.

---

## 1. Neuromorphic chips: which have run language models, at what size, and with what measured efficiency vs. GPUs?

### Takeaway
IBM NorthPole and Intel Loihi 2 have run language models on real hardware. On NorthPole, a 3B model needed 16 cards and an 8B model needed a 288-card, 30 kW rack. On Loihi 2, the model was 370M parameters. Both reported large energy-per-token gains over GPUs at low batch and low latency, but both are research prototypes that keep weights in on-chip SRAM across many chips, so neither is anywhere near phone-sized. The parts built for consumers and edge devices (BrainChip Akida/Akida Pico/Sequora, Innatera Pulsar, SynSense) target milliwatt always-on sensing and at most sub-1B to 3B "LLM" IP, with only preliminary figures. No independent benchmark shows a neuromorphic chip running a useful chat-grade LLM inside a phone's power budget.

### Cited Findings

#### Intel Loihi 2 / Hala Point
- **(older, Apr 17 2024)** Hala Point packs 1,152 Loihi 2 processors (1.15B neurons) into a six-rack-unit chassis "about the size of a microwave oven", with 2,600 W maximum draw. **[CLAIM]**: about 20 petaops peak and over 15 TOPS/W on conventional DNNs. Intel says this preliminary efficiency relies on up to 10:1 sparse connectivity and event-driven activity, and is measured without batching. Deployed at Sandia. — [Intel Newsroom, 17 Apr 2024](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai); [The Register, 17 Apr 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/04/17/intel_hala_point_neuromorphic_owl/)
- Hala Point is a research system, and Loihi 2 is "not yet commercially available". — [HPCwire, Apr 2024](https://www.hpcwire.com/?p=171959)
- **(older, 2021)** Mike Davies (Intel Neuromorphic Lab) said there were no specific plans to commercialize Loihi 2. He suggested the technology would probably first appear as an acceleration core inside an SoC. — [AnandTech interview, Sep/Oct 2021](https://www.anandtech.com/show/16984)
- **LLM on Loihi 2 (Mar 2025, ICLR 2025 workshop; arXiv 2503.18002, "work in progress").** A 370M-parameter MatMul-free (recurrent, ternary-weight) language model ran fully on-chip on Loihi 2. Reported against a transformer LLM on an edge GPU: up to 3x higher throughput with 2x less energy. In v2: prefill at least 2x higher throughput with about 2x less energy per token. Against an H100 running the same model: 3x higher generation throughput and at least 14x less energy per token. The authors attribute the advantage to the recurrent token mixer, whose cost grows linearly, and to weights and state stored locally in the neurocores. Status: preliminary hardware results, read from search excerpts only. — [arXiv 2503.18002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18002); [ICLR 2025 workshop page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/33691)
- **(older, Jun 2024)** The original MatMul-free LM paper (Zhu et al.) reported a Loihi 2 deployment with 4x higher throughput and 10x less energy than edge GPUs. **[MEASURED by authors, small model]** — [arXiv 2406.02528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02528)
- **Loihi 3: unconfirmed.** The only sources found describing a "Loihi 3" (4 nm, about 8M neurons and 64B synapses per chip, 32-bit graded spikes, "released" Jan 2026) are a syndicated "TokenRing" piece and an article whose byline is an AI model. A Russian outlet said there were only rumors in early 2026. No Intel primary source was found. — [Wedbush/TokenRing syndication, 19 Jan 2026](https://investor.wedbush.com/wedbush/article/tokenring-2026-1-19-the-brain-like-revolution-intels-loihi-3-and-the-dawn-of-real-time-neuromorphic-edge-ai); [machineherald, Mar 2026](https://machineherald-io.pages.dev/article/2026-03/23-neuromorphic-computing-crosses-the-production-threshold-as-sandia-solves-physics-on-brain-inspired-hardware); contradicted by [serverflow.ru](https://serverflow.ru/blog/stati/chto-takoe-intel-loihi-razbiraem-semeystvo-neyromorfnykh-protsessorov-ot-intel/)

#### IBM NorthPole
- **(older, Science, 20 Oct 2023, vol. 382, pp. 329–335)** NorthPole is a 12 nm inference chip that interleaves memory and compute. On ResNet-50 it showed 25x higher frames/joule than a GPU on a comparable 12 nm process. **[MEASURED]** — [IBM Research publication page](https://research.ibm.com/publications/neural-inference-at-the-frontier-of-energy-space-and-time)
- Against the 4 nm Nvidia H100 the energy advantage was about 5x, not 25x. IEEE Spectrum also reported it as 22x faster in one-fifth the area. Some press (e.g., TechRadar) dropped the "12 nm GPU" qualifier. — [IEEE Spectrum, Oct 2023](https://spectrum.ieee.org/neuromorphic-computing-ibm-northpole)
- **(older, Sep 2024, IEEE HPEC 2024)** A 3B-parameter LLM (derived from Granite-8B-Code-Base) ran on a 16-card NorthPole appliance: 14 transformer layers on one card each, the output layer on two cards, INT4 weights and KV cache. Results: under 1 ms per token, 28,356 tokens/s system throughput, 46.9x lower latency than the next most energy-efficient GPU, and 72.7x better tokens/s/W than the lowest-latency GPU (H100). The GPU baselines differ by metric (L4 for latency, H100 for energy), and the GPUs are on 4–7 nm vs. NorthPole's 12 nm. **[MEASURED by IBM; not independently verified]** — [IBM Research blog](https://research.ibm.com/blog/northpole-llm-inference-results); [HPEC 2024 paper PDF](https://modha.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/NorthPole_HPEC_LLM_2024.pdf); [modha.org post, Sep 2024](https://modha.org/2024/09/breakthrough-low-latency-high-energy-efficiency-llm-inference-performance-using-northpole)
- **(Nov 2025, arXiv 2511.15950)** In a rack-scale NorthPole system (288 cards in 18 2U servers, 42U, 30 kW, 115 peta-ops INT4, 3.7 PB/s memory bandwidth), three instances of Granite-3.3-8B-instruct ran at 2,048 context with 28 simultaneous users and 2.8 ms per-user inter-token latency. NorthPole is "currently available as a research prototype in a PCIe card form factor". No absolute tokens/joule figure for this rack was found. — [arXiv 2511.15950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15950); [modha.org, Nov 2025](https://modha.org/2025/11/a-scalable-northpole-system-with-end-to-end-vertical-integration-for-low-latency-and-energy-efficient-llm-inference/)

#### BrainChip Akida / Akida Pico / Sequora
- **(older, 1 Oct 2024)** Akida Pico: a co-processor based on Akida2, under 1 mW, 0.12 mm² on GlobalFoundries 22FDX, supporting FP32/INT8/INT4. Targets are voice wake, keyword spotting, noise suppression, presence detection and wearables. It is offered mainly as licensable IP; reports conflict on whether a standalone chip exists. **[CLAIM]** — [BusinessWire press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241001993143/en); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/brainchip-unveils-an-ai-npu-that-consumes-less-than-a-milliwatt)
- BrainChip raised US$25M ahead of CES (for Akida 2 silicon and Akida GenAI models). It showcased a "1.2B LLM on-device in mobile and embedded devices" demo, an open state-space model (TENNs). The Hugging Face model card says "~1B" parameters. — [HPCwire release](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/brainchip-announces-25m-funding-ahead-of-ces-to-power-next-gen-edge-ai/); [Hugging Face tenns-llm-1b](https://huggingface.co/brainchipinc/tenns-llm-1b); [ElectronicsForU](https://www.electronicsforu.com/news/generative-ai-moves-to-battery-powered-edge-devices)
- **(Oct 2026)** Sequora is processor IP for on-device generative AI, scaling from a few million to 3B parameters and designed to pair with Akida for sensing. **[CLAIM, labelled "preliminary" by BrainChip]**: models under 420M parameters run at 20 tokens/s using 4.2 GB/s of memory bandwidth. No milliwatt figure was published for Sequora. — [HPCwire, Oct 2026](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/brainchip-launches-sequora-for-low-power-generative-ai-at-the-edge/); [audioXpress](https://audioxpress.com/news/brainchip-launches-sequora-processor-ip-for-on-device-generative-ai-and-voice-interaction)
- **(Jun 2025, BrainChip conference deck) [SIM/estimate]**: a 1B state-space model at 20 tokens/s needs about 20 GMACs per second, which at 1 pJ/MAC is about 20 mW of compute. The same deck says DDR memory alone costs about 2 W or more, and flash-based weight storage brings active memory power below 0.5 W. This means memory, not compute, dominates power. — [Edge AI & Vision Alliance PDF, Jun 2025](https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/E2W07_Lewis_BrainChip_2025.pdf)

#### SpiNNaker2 / SpiNNcloud (TU Dresden)
- **(Apr 2025)** TU Dresden put a SpiNNcloud system into operation: 35,000 chips and over 5 million processor cores. — [TU Dresden news, 14 Apr 2025](https://tu-dresden.de/tu-dresden/newsportal/news/milestone-for-energy-efficient-ai-systems-tud-launches-spinncloud-supercomputer?set_language=en)
- **[CLAIM]** SpiNNaker2 is "10 times more energy efficient" than high-end GPUs (Arm). Sandia adopted SpiNNaker2 (June 2025), with a company claim of up to 18x GPU efficiency. LLMs are listed as a target workload. The EU-funded "SpiNNext" successor aims at generative AI with hardware support for dynamic sparsity; this is a design goal. — [Arm Newsroom](https://newsroom.arm.com/blog/spinncloud-ai-startup); [PC Watch, Jun 2025](https://pc.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/2021053.html); [CORDIS SpiNNext](https://www.cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101248153)
- **(older, Dec 2023 / IEEE AICAS 2024)** An EGRU-based language model ran on a single SpiNNaker2 chip at batch size 1 using "a fraction of a Watt". The authors report an energy advantage over an Nvidia GPU at batch 1, but GPUs win at larger batches. The LM was memory-limited to batch 1 on one chip. **[MEASURED, small model; exact ratio not retrieved]** — [arXiv 2312.09084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09084)

#### Other neuromorphic / brain-inspired LLM efforts
- **(Aug–Sep 2025)** Zhejiang University's "Darwin Monkey" (Wukong): 960 Darwin 3 chips (15 servers of 64), over 2B neurons, over 100B synapses, about 2,000 W. **[CLAIM]**: it ran a DeepSeek model for logical reasoning, content generation and math. No details were found on how the model was mapped or how fast it ran. — [Zhejiang University, Sep 2025](https://www.zju.edu.cn/english/2025/0910/c19573a3079424/page.htm); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/technology/computing/chinas-darwin-monkey-is-the-worlds-largest-brain-inspired-supercomputer); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3320588/how-chinas-new-darwin-monkey-could-shake-future-ai-world-first)
- **(Sep 2025, revised Dec 2025)** SpikingBrain (Chinese Academy of Sciences): a 7B linear and a 76B hybrid-linear MoE "spiking" LLM, converted from Qwen2.5-7B with about 150B tokens of continual pre-training. **[CLAIM]**: 69.15% activation sparsity and over 100x faster time-to-first-token at 4M-token context. It was trained and evaluated on MetaX GPUs, not on neuromorphic silicon. — [arXiv 2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276)
- **(21 May 2025)** Innatera Pulsar is a neuromorphic MCU (analog and digital SNN blocks, a CNN accelerator and a RISC-V core), available at launch with the Talamo SDK. **[CLAIM]**: up to 100x lower latency and 500x lower energy than conventional AI processors; 600 µW for radar presence detection and 400 µW for audio scene classification. Sensor-edge only, not LLMs. — [EEJournal release](https://www.eejournal.com/industry_news/innatera-unveils-pulsar-the-worlds-first-mass-market-neuromorphic-microcontroller-for-the-sensor-edge/); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/innatera-neuromorphic-chip)
- SynSense and Prophesee: Speck combines a 128×128 event-based vision sensor with SynSense's DYNAP-CNN processor. This is older (2021–22) news; no LLM work was found. Prophesee (event cameras) filed for insolvency in Jan 2025 while continuing operations, later announced a €20M raise and a new CEO (Jean Ferré), and signed an LOI with IDS in Mar 2026. Event cameras are sensors, not LLM compute. — [EE Times Asia](https://www.eetasia.com/?p=33567); [Image Sensors World, 29 Jan 2025](https://image-sensors-world.blogspot.com/2025/01/prophesee-files-for-insolvency.html); [Prophesee news](https://prophesee.ai/stories)

### Inferences
- **The LLMs that have run on neuromorphic hardware are either small or spread across many chips.** NorthPole needed 16 cards for 3B and about 96 cards per 8B instance (288 cards / 3 instances). Loihi 2's LLM result is a 370M model. The architectures win because weights stay in on-chip SRAM, which also means model size scales with chip count. A phone-sized die cannot hold an 8B model's 4–5 GB of INT4 weights in SRAM.
- **The "72.7x" and "14x vs H100" figures are measured at low batch, latency-matched operating points, where GPUs are least efficient.** Rough back-of-envelope (my calculation, high uncertainty): at the 2025 NorthPole rack's figures, if "28 simultaneous users" is the total, that is about 28 × 357 tok/s ≈ 10,000 tok/s for 30 kW, or roughly 3 J/token at rack level. If it means 28 per instance, it is about 1 J/token. Either is far worse than batched-H100 figures in Section 6 (~0.02–0.2 J/token). This suggests NorthPole's main LLM advantage is latency, not throughput-normalized energy. The interpretation is uncertain: whether 30 kW is measured or provisioned, and how users are counted, are both unknown.
- **The neuromorphic IP aimed at consumer silicon is still memory-bound.** BrainChip's own deck shows DDR memory power (~2 W) about 100x larger than compute (~20 mW) for a 1B model at 20 tok/s. Sequora's preliminary figure (420M at 20 tok/s) is far below a capable companion model.
- **Realistic timeline:** neuromorphic co-processors for always-on wake-word, sensing and context detection could appear in phones and wearables now to 2027. A neuromorphic chip running the main conversational LLM of a phone companion is unlikely before about 2028–2030, and no vendor roadmap shows it.

### Gaps
- No primary Intel confirmation of Loihi 3 was found. Specs circulating since Jan 2026 are unverified and may be fabricated by AI-written news sites.
- The number of Loihi 2 chips used for the 370M LLM, and its absolute J/token, were not retrieved (arXiv blocked).
- No measured tokens/s or J/token for Darwin Monkey running DeepSeek, or for SpiNNaker2 running a modern LLM.
- No independent (non-vendor) benchmark of any neuromorphic chip on an LLM was found.
- No phone or laptop design win was found for BrainChip, Innatera or SynSense LLM IP.

## 2. Analog / in-memory computing (IBM PCM, Mythic, EnCharge, Sagence, RRAM, Samsung/SK hynix PIM): accuracy, noise and LLM results

### Takeaway
Analog in-memory chips have shown measured, accuracy-preserving inference on CNNs, RNNs and speech (IBM 2023), and IBM has run a transformer on analog tiles. All published LLM-scale analog results are simulations (IBM 3D-analog MoE, 2025). Device noise and limited precision still cause large accuracy losses on transformers. Commercial analog parts (EnCharge EN100, Mythic) are just reaching market with vendor-only efficiency claims. The near-term consumer win is digital processing-in-memory (PIM) DRAM. Samsung's LPDDR5X-PIM showed a preliminary 3.01x tokens/s gain on Llama 3.1 8B (Aug 2026), and JEDEC LPDDR6-PIM is nearing finalization, with AI-PC adoption reported as early as 2027.

### Cited Findings

#### IBM analog AI (phase-change memory)
- **(older, Nature Electronics, Aug 2023)** IBM's "Hermes" mixed-signal chip has 64 analog in-memory cores, each a 256×256 PCM crossbar. It reached 92.81% on CIFAR-10. **[MEASURED]** — [IBM Research blog](https://research.ibm.com/blog/analog-ai-chip-inference)
- **(older, Nature, 23 Aug 2023, vol. 620 pp. 768–775)** A 14 nm chip with 35M PCM cells in 34 tiles, peak 12.4 TOPS/W. On Google Speech Commands it ran 7x faster at comparable accuracy. On Librispeech it was 14x more energy efficient, which IBM's blog calls an *estimate*. Earlier simulations had projected 40–140x over GPUs. — [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06337-5); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/analog-ai-ibm); [IBM blog](https://researcher.ibm.com/blog/analog-ai-chip-low-power)
- IBM reports the first deployment of a transformer on an analog in-memory chip, with all static-weight matrix-vector multiplications in analog, plus PCM work for compact edge models. The exact date was not confirmed in the search excerpt. — [IBM Research blog](https://researcher.ibm.com/blog/how-can-analog-in-memory-computing-power-transformer-models)
- **(Nature Computational Science, 2025; DOI 10.1038/s43588-024-00753-x) [SIM]**: mapping each MoE expert to a separate layer of a 3D non-volatile memory stack gave higher throughput, area efficiency and energy efficiency than commercial GPUs on the same MoE model, based on simulation and benchmarking only. — [IBM publication page](https://www.research.ibm.com/publications/efficient-scaling-of-large-language-models-with-mixture-of-experts-and-3d-analog-in-memory-computing)

#### Startups
- **(29 May 2025)** EnCharge AI EN100 uses charge-domain analog in-memory compute (metal-wire capacitors) plus RISC-V cores. The M.2 laptop card is **[CLAIM]** 200+ TOPS at 8.25 W. The PCIe card is about 1 POPS at up to 40 W, with 16 nm CMOS and 128 GB on-board memory. EnCharge claims up to 20x better performance per watt than competitors. No laptop OEM design win was announced. — [BusinessWire, 29 May 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250529108055/en); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/encharge-ai); [EN100 product page](https://en100.enchargeai.com/llms.txt)
- **(17 Dec 2025)** Mythic raised an oversubscribed $125M (led by DCVC). **[CLAIM]**: a "100x energy advantage" over GPUs, and analog chiplets that "support 1T+ parameter LLMs in data centers". No independent LLM benchmark was found. — [BusinessWire, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251217546249/en/Mythic-to-Challenge-AIs-GPU-Pantheon-with-100x-Energy-Advantage-and-Oversubscribed-$125M-Raise)
- **(older, Nov 2024)** Sagence AI (formerly Analog Inference) planned chips for 2025. Its first product targets vision, with LLM-class 3D-stacked analog chips (UCIe) as a later goal. — [TechCrunch, 18 Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/18/sagence-is-building-analog-chips-to-run-ai/)

#### RRAM / memristor crossbars
- **(older, Nature, Aug 2022)** NeuRRAM, a 48-core RRAM compute-in-memory chip, achieved fully hardware-measured accuracy comparable to 4-bit-quantized software on CNN and RNN benchmarks. It was not a transformer or LLM. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385482)
- **(2025 preprint)** Transformers suffer "catastrophic accuracy loss" when implemented entirely with 2-bit multi-level-cell RRAM, e.g., a 40% drop for BERT-Base on GLUE MRPC. The proposed fix is a hybrid SLC/MLC mapping, from search excerpt only. — [arXiv 2506.00020](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00020v1)
- **(Mar 2026 preprint)** Memristor non-idealities degrade LLM reasoning. **[SIM]** — [arXiv 2603.13725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13725)

#### Processing-in-memory DRAM (Samsung / SK hynix)
- **(Dec 2024)** Samsung and SK hynix agreed to jointly standardize LPDDR6-PIM for on-device AI through JEDEC. — [TrendForce, 2 Dec 2024](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2024/12/02/news-samsung-and-sk-hynix-reportedly-unite-to-standardize-lpddr6-pim-for-on-device-ai/)
- **(May 2026)** The JEDEC LPDDR6-PIM spec was reported close to completion, with Samsung samples planned for 2H 2026. — [Newstomato, May 2026](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=1300001)
- **(Hot Chips 2026, 25 Aug 2026)** Samsung LPDDR5X-PIM. **[preliminary, vendor]**: Llama 3.1 8B showed 3.01x tokens/s and a 2.28x model-runtime improvement over plain LPDDR5X. Internal bandwidth is about 614 GB/s vs. 76.8 GB/s at the external x64 9600 Mbps interface, about 8x. Targets mobile/client edge AI. No LPDDR5X-PIM energy-saving percentage was published; older HBM-PIM energy figures should not be transferred to it. — [Tom's Hardware, Aug 2026](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/hot-chips-2026-samsung-makes-lpddr5x-smart-with-logic-unit-in-memory-lpddr5x-pim-is-3-01x-faster-than-lpddr5x-in-ai-inference-with-8x-the-bandwidth); [ServeTheHome, Aug 2026](https://www.servethehome.com/samsung-lpddr5x-pim-at-hot-chips-2026/)
- **(26 Aug 2026, headline only)** TrendForce: "Samsung's 4nm GAIA could mark first PIM commercialization in AI PCs; mass production as early as 2027." Only the headline was seen. — [TrendForce, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/08/26/news-samsungs-4nm-gaia-could-mark-first-pim-commercialization-in-ai-pcs-mass-production-as-early-as-2027/)
- SK hynix's AiM (Accelerator-in-Memory) is GDDR6-based. Its AiMX card offloads memory-bound LLM work from GPUs in data centers (AI Infra Summit 2025; CES 2026), and LPDDR-AiM is listed as an on-device direction. — [SK hynix News, 2025](https://news.skhynix.com/ai-infra-summit-2025/); [SK hynix Hot Chips 2024 slides](https://www.hc2024.hotchips.org/assets/program/conference/day1/11_HC2024.SKhynix.GuhyunKim.rev920240822.pdf)

### Inferences
- Of all approaches in these notes, PIM attacks the real bottleneck most directly, since decode moves every weight across the DRAM bus per token (Section 5). Samsung's x64 LPDDR5X external figure (76.8 GB/s) is in the same range as flagship phone bandwidth today. An ~8x internal-bandwidth path explains the reported ~3x decode gain on an 8B model. If SoC vendors adopt it, laptops from about 2027 and phones from about 2027–2028 could see 2–3x faster local decoding at similar or lower energy. This depends on OS, runtime and SoC support, none of which was confirmed.
- Analog matrix multiply offers big theoretical efficiency gains, but LLMs need high-precision, frequently updated activations and KV caches. Published transformer and LLM analog results are either simulation or show large noise-driven accuracy losses. A consumer analog LLM accelerator with measured, accuracy-matched chat-model results was not found. The EnCharge EN100 M.2 is the closest candidate but has no public LLM benchmark.

### Gaps
- No independent tokens/s or J/token for EN100, Mythic or Sagence on any LLM.
- Samsung's LPDDR5X-PIM energy figures and which SoC partners will adopt it were not found. GAIA details are known only from a headline.
- No 2025–2026 *Nature*-family paper was found showing an RRAM/memristor chip running a full transformer LLM on hardware with accuracy parity.

## 3. Photonic computing (Lightmatter, Lightelligence, Q.ANT, academic): what has actually been demonstrated?

### Takeaway
Two April 2025 *Nature* papers showed real photonic-electronic processors. Lightmatter ran ResNet, BERT and Atari RL on four 128×128 photonic tensor cores. Lightelligence's PACE (64×64) targeted ultra-low-latency optimization and Ising problems. Neither demonstrated a generative LLM. Lightmatter's commercial products are optical interconnects. Q.ANT sells data-center photonic NPU servers with vendor efficiency claims. Photonics has no plausible path into a phone or laptop for running a companion model in the next few years.

### Cited Findings
- **(9 Apr 2025, Nature 640, 368–374; Lightmatter)** "Universal photonic AI acceleration": four 128×128 photonic matrices; 65.5 trillion ABFP16 ops/s at 78 W electrical plus 1.6 W optical. It ran ResNet, BERT and Atari reinforcement learning. Lightmatter says unmodified models run at "near FP32 accuracy" without retraining. **Conflict:** Heise reports about 90% of conventional accuracy on image classification but "just under 30%" on natural-language questions, so the BERT accuracy claim needs checking against the paper. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08854-x); [Lightmatter blog](https://lightmatter.co/blog/a-new-kind-of-computer/); contradicted by [heise, Apr 2025](https://heise.de/-10354733); [Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/photonic-computer-chips-perform-as-well-as-purely-electronic-counterparts-say-researchers/)
- **(9 Apr 2025, Nature 640, 361–367; Lightelligence)** PACE: over 16,000 photonic components in a 64×64 matrix, up to 1 GHz, minimum latency up to 500x lower than smaller circuits, and Ising problems solved in about 5 ns. It is a hybrid photonic-electronic system aimed at optimization, not LLMs. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v640y2025i8058d10.1038_s41586-025-08786-6.html); [Nature Asia press summary](https://www.natureasia.com/ja-jp/nature/pr-highlights/15210); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/computer-chips-math-light-photonic)
- **(31 Mar–1 Apr 2025)** Lightmatter's shipping products are interconnects. Passage M1000 is a 3D photonic interposer with 114 Tbps total optical bandwidth, available summer 2025. Passage L200 is an optical chiplet due in 2026. — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250331220170/en/Lightmatter-Unveils-Passage-M1000-Photonic-Superchip-Worlds-Fastest-AI-Interconnect); [The Register, 1 Apr 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/01/lightmatter_photonics_passage/)
- **(Nov 2024 to Mar 2026)** Q.ANT launched its first commercial photonic NPU (Nov 2024) and deployed one at LRZ (Jul 2025). NPU 2 / NPS 2 servers were announced at SC25 (Nov 2025), with shipments in H1 2026 (one source says Q1 2026), and second-generation processors were deployed at LRZ in Mar 2026. **[CLAIM]**: up to 90x lower power per workload and about 30 W per unit. An analyst calls it "a real but narrow beachhead". No LLM benchmark was found. — [Quantum Insider, 20 Nov 2024](https://thequantuminsider.com/2024/11/20/q-ant-launches-first-commercial-photonic-processor/); [Optica OPN, Jul 2025](https://www.optica-opn.org/home/industry/2025/july/german_supercomputing_center_deploys_world_s_first_photonic_ai_processor/); [Quantum Insider, 19 Nov 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/11/19/qant-next-gen-photonic-npu/); [Photonics Online, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.photonicsonline.com/doc/higher-performance-less-energy-q-ant-deploys-second-generation-photonic-processors-0001); [Jon Peddie Research](https://www.jonpeddie.com/news/remember-optical-computing-its-here/)

### Inferences
- Photonics speeds up matrix multiplies, but LLM decoding on a single device is limited by moving weights from memory, not by multiply speed. A photonic MAC array does not remove the weight-fetch cost, and adds laser, modulator and converter overheads. The industry's revealed preference (Lightmatter shipping interconnect, Q.ANT targeting HPC and data centers) points to photonics helping cloud-scale AI first. Consumer-device photonic AI before 2030 looks unlikely.

### Gaps
- Exact BERT/ResNet accuracy numbers from the Lightmatter paper could not be verified (nature.com blocked), and the Heise vs. Lightmatter conflict is unresolved.
- No photonic system has published tokens/s or J/token on any generative LLM.

## 4. Biological / organoid computing (FinalSpark, Cortical Labs CL1, Brainoware, Johns Hopkins OI): real capabilities vs. hype, and ethics

### Takeaway
Biological computing is a real, purchasable research tool: the Cortical Labs CL1 costs about $35k (or $300 per week in the cloud), and FinalSpark rents remote access. Demonstrated tasks are small: closed-loop game play (Pong, a Doom demo), Japanese-vowel speaker ID at 78% (worse than ANNs) and chaotic time-series prediction. No organoid system has run, or credibly approximates, a language model. Cell cultures live about 100 days to 6 months and need incubators and life support. Energy claims ("a million times less power") are company assertions that ignore the life-support overhead. Not viable for a phone or laptop companion in any foreseeable timeframe. Governance and ethics frameworks are still being proposed.

### Cited Findings
- **(older, Nature Electronics, Dec 2023)** Brainoware used a brain organoid as a reservoir computer. It identified speakers from 240 clips of Japanese vowels (8 speakers), with accuracy improving from 51% to 78% over training. That is less accurate than conventional ANNs, though it predicted a Hénon map better than an ANN baseline. The lead author called it a proof of concept. **[MEASURED]** — [Nature Electronics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41928-023-01069-w); [TechXplore, 12 Dec 2023](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-12-brain-tissue-chip-voice-recognition.html)
- **(Frontiers in AI, 2024, and 2025 coverage)** FinalSpark Neuroplatform: organoids last over 100 days. The platform houses 2,000–3,000 organoids, with 4 per processing unit (16 in the original setup). **[CLAIM]**: bioprocessors use "a million times less power" than digital processors. Pricing conflicts: $500 per user per month for education (Tom's Hardware) vs. $1,000 per month per cluster (National Geographic). — [Frontiers in AI paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frai.2024.1376042); [Scientific American](https://scientificamerican.com/article/these-living-computers-are-made-from-human-neurons); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/worlds-first-bioprocessor-uses-16-human-brain-organoids-for-a-million-times-less-power-consumption-than-a-digital-chip); [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/brain-cells-organoids-computers-ai-energy)
- **(2 Mar 2025, MWC Barcelona)** Cortical Labs CL1 launched: about $35,000 per unit, or about $20,000 per unit in a 30-unit rack, with shipping from mid/late 2025. Neurons have a reported 6-month lifespan. Buyers need institutional ethics approval and cell-culture facilities. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/biological-computer-for-sale); [heise](https://heise.de/-10312614); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/brain/cortical-bioengineered-intelligence)
- Cloud "wetware-as-a-service" costs $300 per week per unit. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/what-a-deal-you-can-now-rent-human-brain-cells-on-a-silicon-chip-for-7-days-for-less-than-a-nintendo-switch-2)
- **(Feb–Mar 2026)** A CL1 with about 200,000 human neurons was shown "playing Doom", and a hackathon developer did this via the cloud system. These are demos, not peer-reviewed benchmarks. — [Habr](https://habr.com/ru/articles/1008890); [ConnectSci, Mar 2026](https://connectsci.au/news/news-parent/7947/Biocomputer-with-live-neurons-brought-to-life); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/111506-35000-computer-made-living-human-neurons-can-run.html)
- **Johns Hopkins "Organoid Intelligence" (older, Frontiers in Science, 2023)** is a roadmap paper defining OI as a field. It includes an "embedded ethics" approach. — [Frontiers in Science, DOI 10.3389/fsci.2023.1068159](https://doi.org/10.3389/fsci.2023.1068159)
- **Ethics and hype:** OI researchers (Hartung, Morales Pantoja, Smirnova and others) argue a proactive governance framework is needed. Other researchers warn that claiming cultures in closed-loop systems show "sentience" or "intelligence" distorts public perception. Cortical Labs presents its work as an "ethically superior alternative to animal testing" and requires customers to obtain ethics approval. — [Frontiers in AI review](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1307613/full); [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10796793); [Neuroethics Society](https://neuroethicssociety.org/?p=3132)

### Inferences
- The "million-times" energy claims compare neurons' intrinsic signalling energy against digital chips. They exclude incubators, perfusion, multi-electrode arrays and the electronic readout, so the systems as built are not low-power end to end. No path from these systems to running a transformer LLM was found.
- For a personal companion, the relevant lesson is conceptual (brains are event-driven, sparse and adaptive), not practical. Organoid hardware is a decade-plus research bet with unresolved consent, sentience and welfare questions.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed quantitative comparison of task accuracy per joule for organoid systems vs. silicon, including life-support energy, was found.
- No 2026 Johns Hopkins or major-body ethics guidance was found beyond the 2023–2024 papers.

## 5. Consumer device reality today: memory bandwidth, NPUs, why decoding is bandwidth-bound, tokens/s for 1B–8B models, battery and thermal limits

### Takeaway
Flagship phones in 2025–2026 have about 60–85 GB/s of LPDDR5X bandwidth and 12–24 GB of RAM. Laptops range from 120–228 GB/s (base M-series, Snapdragon X2) to over 600 GB/s (M5 Max). Single-stream decoding reads every weight once per token, so tokens/s ≈ effective bandwidth ÷ model bytes. llama.cpp measurements on Apple silicon track this almost linearly. In practice, 1B–3B 4-bit models run at about 20–58 tok/s on flagship-phone NPUs and GPUs (vendor figures) and degrade 15–50% under sustained thermal load. 7–8B models are comfortable on laptops (about 24–32 tok/s on base M4/M5) but marginal on phones. A private offline companion is therefore feasible *today* with conventional NPUs and GPUs at the 3B–8B scale, without brain-like hardware.

### Cited Findings

#### Apple
- **(15 Oct 2025)** M5: 153 GB/s unified memory bandwidth (about 30% over M4's 120 GB/s), with Neural Accelerators added to every GPU core alongside a 16-core Neural Engine. — [Apple Newsroom, 15 Oct 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/10/apple-unleashes-m5-the-next-big-leap-in-ai-performance-for-apple-silicon/)
- **(Nov 2025)** Apple ML Research (via 9to5Mac): local LLM token generation is 19–27% faster on M5 than M4, attributed to the extra memory bandwidth. Time-to-first-token is up to 3.6x faster, a compute gain from the GPU neural accelerators. — [9to5Mac, 20 Nov 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/20/apple-shows-how-much-faster-the-m5-runs-local-llms-compared-to-the-m4/)
- A19 Pro (iPhone 17 Pro): about 76 GB/s, vs. about 60 GB/s for the A18 Pro. — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2942646/apples-m5-mac-chip-is-just-a-big-a19-pro-and-thats-a-good-thing.html)
- **(older, Jun 2024)** Apple's ~3B on-device model ran at 30 tok/s generation on an iPhone 15 Pro, with 0.6 ms per prompt token time-to-first-token, using a mixed 2/4-bit scheme averaging 3.7 bits per weight. **[MEASURED by vendor]** — [Apple ML Research, Jun 2024](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models)

#### Thermal and battery limits
- **(Mar 2026 preprint)** Under sustained LLM load over 20 warm iterations, throughput degraded about 15% on a Galaxy S24 Ultra and about 41.5% on an iPhone. ### Inferences
- **Decoding is bandwidth-bound (my calculation from the llama.cpp table).** A Llama-2-7B Q4_0 file is about 3.8 GB (≈6.7B params × ~4.5 bits). Theoretical ceilings are then about 31 tok/s (M4, 120 GB/s), 40 (M5, 154 GB/s) and 160 (M5 Max, 614 GB/s). Measured values (24.1, 31.9, 119.9) are about 75–80% of these in every case. Throughput scales with bandwidth, not with TOPS.
- **Phone ceilings (my calculation).** A 64-bit LPDDR5X-10667 bus gives about 85 GB/s, consistent with the "84.8" spec-sheet figure and Samsung's 76.8 GB/s x64 PIM baseline. The 228 GB/s sometimes attributed to the 8 Elite Gen 5 appears to be the X2 Elite Extreme laptop figure. At 85 GB/s, the bandwidth-bound ceilings are about 45 tok/s for a 3B 4-bit model (~1.8 GB) and about 17–19 tok/s for an 8B 4-bit model (~4.5–5 GB). Qualcomm's 57.8 tok/s for the "SSD" 3B variant exceeds this. I infer (unverified) that SSD means self-speculative decoding, which produces multiple tokens per weight pass.
- **What is achievable now for a companion:** on phones, a 1B–3B model at about 20–50 tok/s (peak), falling 15–50% when sustained, with 3–4B as the practical sweet spot. 7–8B is possible at about 10–18 tok/s peak but uses much of the 12–16 GB RAM and throttles. On laptops, 8B runs comfortably (about 24–32 tok/s on base M4/M5, 50–120 on Pro/Max), and 30B-class models fit on 32–64 GB machines. 2-bit/1.58-bit quantization (Apple 2025, MediaTek BitNet) and weight compression are the levers vendors are using to beat the bandwidth wall.

### Gaps
- Tensor G5 and A19 Pro bandwidth figures are not official from Google or Apple (A19 Pro from Macworld only).
- No independent absolute tokens/s for Dimensity 9500 or Tensor G5 on a named open model.
- No MLPerf Mobile/Client LLM results were retrieved in this session.
- The exact meaning of Qualcomm's "SSD" variant and its quality trade-off was not confirmed.

## 6. Energy per token: GPUs vs. phones vs. neuromorphic chips

### Takeaway
Measured energy per token spans orders of magnitude and depends mostly on batch size and model size. Batched data-center GPUs reach about 0.02 J/token (1B) to about 0.2 J/token (7B) on the GPU alone. Phones use about 0.26–0.67 J/token for small models (PowerInfer-2 data). One 2026 study found mobile inference *less* energy-efficient per token than a batched server, but *more* efficient than an unbatched server. Neuromorphic chips claim 14–73x better tokens per joule than GPUs only at low-batch, low-latency operating points, on small models or very large multi-chip systems. No neuromorphic device has a measured J/token for an 8B-class model in a phone power envelope.

### Cited Findings
- **GPU (H100, TensorRT-LLM, batched) [MEASURED]**: Llama 3.2 1B ≈ 55.91 tokens/J (≈0.018 J/token) and Falcon-RW 7B ≈ 5.12 tokens/J (≈0.2 J/token), computed from average GPU power over request count. — [arXiv 2611.03330 "SIMPLE Law", Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03330)
- On 4×H100, going from LLaMA-3 1B to 70B raised energy per token about 7.3x despite 70x more parameters. — [TokenPowerBench, arXiv 2512.03024, Dec 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03024)
- Scheduling, batching and quantization changes cut per-request energy by up to 100x for Llama 3.1 8B on H100. — [arXiv 2601.22362, Jan 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22362v1)
- Decode is more energy-intensive per token than prefill for Llama-3.1-8B. — [arXiv 2504.17674, Apr 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.17674)
- **Phones [MEASURED] (older, Jun 2024)**: PowerInfer-2 uses 0.257 J/token, vs. 0.373 J/token for Qualcomm QNN and 0.672 J/token for llama.cpp, on a smartphone. These are excerpt figures; the model and device were not confirmed in this session. — [arXiv 2406.06282](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.06282)
- **Phone vs. server (Sep 2026)**: mobile inference is less energy- and time-efficient than batched remote servers. Moving from a Pixel 8 to a batched server saves about 65% energy per token; from an iPhone 14, about 48%. Without batching, the server is *less* energy-efficient than the phones. — [arXiv 2609.11940 "The Battery Price of edge AI"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11940)
- **Neuromorphic**: NorthPole showed 72.7x better tokens/s/W than an H100 at the H100's lowest-latency point (3B model, 16 cards, 2024). — [IBM Research blog](https://research.ibm.com/blog/northpole-llm-inference-results)
- Loihi 2 showed at least 14x less energy per token than an H100, and about 2x less than an edge GPU, on a 370M MatMul-free model (2025, preliminary). — [arXiv 2503.18002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18002)
- **[SIM]** BrainChip estimates about 20 mW of compute for a 1B model at 20 tok/s (≈1 mJ/token compute only), but about 2 W of DDR memory power (≈0.1 J/token at the system level). — [BrainChip deck, Jun 2025](https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/E2W07_Lewis_BrainChip_2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **At batch size 1 on device, memory traffic dominates energy.** A phone at about 0.25–0.7 J/token running at about 20 tok/s draws roughly 5–14 W, which matches the thermal throttling seen in Section 5. This is why PIM (fewer DRAM bus crossings) and compute-near-memory designs are the most relevant "non-conventional" directions for a phone companion.
- **Neuromorphic results are not comparable like-for-like.** Their multipliers are against low-batch GPU configurations, and at the system level, holding all weights in on-chip SRAM needs many chips. Per-user energy for a single private user at batch 1 is where neuromorphic and in-memory designs could win in principle. That is the companion use case, but no device has shown it at 3B+ parameters in a handheld power budget.
- **Bottom line for the end use:** a private offline companion on a 2025–2026 flagship phone or laptop is feasible today with conventional NPUs and GPUs and aggressive quantization (3–8B models). Brain-like hardware is not needed for this and cannot yet deliver it. The most likely near-term efficiency gains (2027–2028) come from LPDDR6 and PIM memory, low-bit (2-bit/ternary) models and possibly neuromorphic always-on co-processors for sensing and wake-up. Neuromorphic main-LLM inference on a phone stays speculative.

### Gaps
- No standardized cross-platform J/token benchmark spans GPU, phone NPU and neuromorphic hardware on the same model.
- Absolute J/token figures for the 2025 NorthPole rack, the Loihi 2 LLM and any Apple M5 or iPhone LLM run were not found.
- The PowerInfer-2 model and device details and the absolute J/token numbers from arXiv 2609.11940 could not be read in full (arxiv.org blocked).
